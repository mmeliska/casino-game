## Time at the table

Time runs on what you do, not the real clock, so you can play one game for hours without running through the story. Settled with Mike on 2026-10-19.

- **Rounds and the clock:** each hand, spin or roll moves the in-game clock a few minutes, but only up to the end of the current part of the day. After that the clock stops and you can keep playing as long as you like.
- **Table events:** these fire at random every 40 to 80 rounds or so, at most two or three per sitting before things go quiet. They're small breaks in the action and don't move the main story (see the next section).
- **Story beats:** these never fire mid-game. They wait for a break point: getting up from the table, walking into a venue, waking up, or crossing the river.
- **Why the story can't be ground through:** a long session can raise your best bankroll, but act gates also need days to pass, and days pass only when you move, sleep or travel.
- **Closing the app:** no in-game time passes while it's shut. You come back to the same seat, and small timers count in-game time only.
- **Changing casino:** travel takes up part of the day, and arriving is a break point.
- **Pacing:** the "about 15 minutes per day" figure is for normal play that moves around the city. Long sessions at one game add hours on top.
