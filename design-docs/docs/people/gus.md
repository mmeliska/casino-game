*Core · the Levee · Starflare floor and office; coffee under the rocket sign*

## Look

Very heavyset (Teddy from Bob's Burgers without the beanie, as an artist reference only), silver hair combed back, his dad's old Starflare dinner jacket straining at the buttons, a chipped coffee mug with the rocket logo.

## Backstory

Late 50s, the son of the founder, Mr. Hamilton. His father built the Starflare in the rocket age; Gus grew up in the place and inherited it with its debts and secrets. His late wife's touches are all over the Flare, which is why he can't let go; she was Dot's sister. He is secretly a gambler in the Cinder Room, and Wren's cameras see all of it. Years ago, drunk, he lost the staff payroll at Rosa's table and let her take the fall. The commission's penalty for that night ordered a modern camera system he couldn't afford. Wren offered to install hers for a fraction of the cost, and when he refused she showed him Cinder Room footage of his gambling and threatened to send it to Stella and the commission, so he signed knowing it was Revaris; when Bennett found out, Gus had to wipe out Richard's remaining markers, and he gave Richard the key to a box holding the proof (M4). Wren's cameras cost him far less than they were worth because the real payment is the data.

## Personality

Proud, sentimental, stubborn, bad with money.

## Story

You meet him on your first Flare visit (coffee, tapping the rocket's fin) and care builds slowly from small things. Ray collects on his debt, a "friend of Ray's" buys the Flare and clears his debt (really Revaris Technologies; Gus signs knowing it's the mob and, ashamed, keeps the buyer secret. Revaris then puts the land into the Levee Stadium District, with Bennett pushing it through). Once sold, it's sold: winning the Starflare name back for him by white-hat leverage is act 4. His trust is ordinary NPC standing plus a few flag checks.

## Voice

Long, warm, nostalgic sentences that trail off. Talks about "my dad's place"; never says the word "debt". Lies by changing the subject to the old days. *"You should've seen this floor when the band played. Anyway. Coffee?"*
