## Summary
The cast already shares a wound nobody has named: a place or thing inherited from a father, kept alive past its time or lost at a table. Name it as the story's theme, list the father-objects, and give writers one rule: when a scene is about the Flare, it is also about whose house it is.

## Current state
Gus wears his father's jacket and keeps "my dad's place" (people/gus.md). Bennett's father lost his house to the Flare (people/bennett.md). Richard taught his son War and lost the house (people/richard.md). Kasia wears her dad's union pin and runs his bar (people/kasia.md). Pete's pin is from Gus's father (people/pete.md). Tae's father drummed in the Flare's band (people/tae.md). Wren took the reins from her grandfather (people/wren.md). Vivian's fortune is her great-grandfather's and her family owns the land (people/vivian.md; the grandfather's handshake is S-033's proposal). Jalen bused tables where Pete started. The docs use "the house" for the casino, Gus's family and Richard's home (pages/review.md). No page states the theme.

## Proposed change
**The theme, in one line for writers:** *Everyone in Northlight is living in someone else's house, and the game asks what you owe the person who built it.*

**The father-objects (inserts; the ones in parentheses are proposed in this set, and the theme stands without any one of them, so they are not listed as dependencies):** Gus's jacket and mug; Kasia's pin; Pete's pin; Richard's deck; Bennett's desk photo; Tae's father's kit (S-036); Vivian's handshake and pearls (S-033); Wren's third bandit (S-042); Jalen's brother's route (S-027); the rival's watch (S-030); Mitch's storefront sticker (S-051); Dot's shirt (S-017). Each is a thing from an older generation that the character can't put down.

**The rule in play:** every ending answers the question for the Flare. O1: Gus keeps his father's house by letting it become something else. O2: you take it. O4: the regulars make it theirs. O5: Tae hangs his father's photo in it. O6: Gus puts it down. O7: Bennett puts a dome over the lot where his father lost his. O8: Wren adds it to her grandfather's room. O9: it burns. The epilogue's Starflare panel can carry one line that says which.

**The word.** "The house" is already a double meaning in the docs; make it the game's one deliberate repeated word, used sparingly: the casino's house, Richard's house, "the house always wins" said once by Kasia about the Flare and meaning Gus's family. (Not Rosa: her rule against the word breaks only once, in S-021.)

## Why it works
A theme the writers can name is the difference between a cast of people with secrets and a story. This one is already there in every character sheet; naming it costs nothing and makes a dozen suggestions in this set (S-011, S-016, S-024, S-030, S-033, S-036, S-040, S-051) pull in the same direction. It also tells the player, without saying so, why a blank-slate stranger would fight for a dying casino: because they're the only one in the city who doesn't have a father's house, and they get to choose one.

## How the player experiences it
Never as a line of text. As the inserts, the word "house" heard in two senses, and the sense at the end that the endings were all answers to the same question.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Themes announced become sermons; this one should live in the writing guidelines and nowhere on screen. The "stranger with no house" reading of the player should never be spoken either.
