## Summary
Name the thing Juan's story already is: he keeps a dying place open with illegal money and tells himself it's maintenance. Give him one scene where he says so, and make the Tuesday league's shirts (S1) his version of the Flare's regulars.

## Current state
Juan keeps the ancient pinsetters running and runs dice in the back lot after the last frame; "the owner looks away because the dice money pays for parts"; fair game; Umbra tries to muscle in (R3); the league is $40 short for shirts (S1); he tells you about the Flare at A1.3 (people/juan.md, plan/t09.md). No stated want beyond the machines.

## Proposed change
**Want:** the pinsetters running one more season. **Need:** to admit the Lanes are a casino with a bowling problem.

**The mirror, said once.** The first time you come back to the Lanes after meeting Gus, in the back lot after the last frame (he never says "dice" inside the alley), he has a line he's never said to anyone: "Gus runs a casino that wishes it was a supper club. I run a bowling alley that's a dice game. Same thing. Don't tell him I said so." That's his whole opinion of the Levee.

**R3 sharpened.** When Umbra's loaded dice come, what's at stake is stated in parts: "They take the late game, the parts money goes with it. Lane six is dead by the first snow." Pushing them out saves a lane. If the player fails, lane six goes dark, and the league bowls on five.

**S1 extended.** The league in their shirts at the council hearing (A3.2) are the Lanes' regulars standing up for the Flare's. Juan doesn't come; he's under a machine. If you ask why: "Somebody's gotta keep the lights on where I am."

**Badly:** if the player brings heat to the Lanes (a raid traced to you), the late game stops, the parts stop, and the next time you come the alley has a sign in the window about the pinsetters. He's still there. He doesn't say "Couldn't tell you." He says nothing.

## Why it works
The cheapest mirror in the game: two men keeping their fathers' and bosses' places alive on money they don't talk about, on opposite banks. One line from Juan makes the player see the Flare from Backwater. The lane as a stake gives R3 a loss the player can see from the door.

## How the player experiences it
Three lines and a plate state (lane six dark).

## Risks and tradeoffs
Nearly free. Keep Juan's line to one; he is "indifferent to everyone else" and should stay that way.
