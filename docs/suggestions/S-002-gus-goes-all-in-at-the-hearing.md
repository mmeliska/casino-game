## Summary
At the license hearing (A5.7), before the hard question, Gus asks to speak and tells the commission about the payroll night himself, to restore Rosa's license. The player's choice is whether to let him. It is the first time in the game Gus says the word "debt".

## Current state
A5.7 (pages/storymap-beats.md) is a test of the player's record; it names the possible speakers and the hard question, and doesn't place Gus in the room at all. This suggestion puts him there. M1's payoff (plan/t08.md): "Gus admits it; Pete and Gus testify and Rosa gets her license back." Gus's voice sheet (people/gus.md): "never says the word 'debt'; lies by changing the subject to the old days." Gus's arc so far is confession under pressure (A4.1) and being helped (A4.2); he never chooses a cost for himself.

## Proposed change
When the commissioner opens the floor, Gus stands. Two-shot, then a pose swap to a pose we have not seen: chin up, mug left on the table. "There's a dealer who lost her license on my floor. She was dealing me back what I'd lost. The debt was mine." (He says the word. Rosa, if present, looks at her hands.) The player has a stake choice before he finishes:

- **Let him.** Rosa's license is restored at the hearing whether or not M1 was solved (if it was, Pete's testimony makes it faster and cleaner). The new license carries a condition on Gus: he can host but not hold a stake for the first year. This leans O1 toward "Gus cuts the ribbon as the host, not the owner", which is a warmer reading of the existing ending, not a new one.
- **Stop him.** "Let the old days stay old." Gus sits. Rosa's license still comes back later through M1 if you solved it; if you didn't, it doesn't. Gus is grateful and smaller for it, and the cool-off afterwards reads as shame, not anger.
- **Say it for him.** You tell the commission in his place, in plain words. Gus is spared and furious; Rosa is restored; Gus's standing dips and recovers only if you never bring it up again.

## Why it works
A character who only ever has things done to him can't carry an ending. Giving Gus one active sacrifice, at a cost to the thing he loves, is the turn his arc is missing (vulnerability, then agency). Letting the player decide whether to permit it keeps the climax the player's while making Gus someone worth cutting a ribbon with. The forbidden word from the voice sheet becomes a payoff instead of a rule.

## How the player experiences it
Hearing-room scene; Gus's new pose is the only new art. The choice is timed by the panel sequence rather than a timer: you choose between his second and third lines. Rosa's pose swap and silence are the only reaction. On opening night, if you let him speak, Rosa deals the first blackjack hand on the new floor and Gus sits at it with a coffee and does not play.

## Risks and tradeoffs
It takes the climax's active choice away from the player and gives it to Gus; S-001 does the reverse. Doing both in one hearing would blunt each. If Gus is absent or hostile (the O2 track), the beat doesn't fire and the hearing runs as the docs have it. Players who never found M1 get the payroll story cold here, so Gus's speech must carry the facts in three panels. The "not hold a stake" condition touches the backers rule only in flavour (Jalen and Vivian are still the only backers; Gus never was).

## Decision notes
- Mike, 2026-10-27: rejected. The hearing belongs to the player (S-001, rewritten around the camera deal). Gus is never on the new Starflare's license, so the "host but not hold a stake" condition no longer applies; he retires, and in O1 he cuts the ribbon as a guest.
