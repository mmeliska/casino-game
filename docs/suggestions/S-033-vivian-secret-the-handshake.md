## Summary
Vivian isn't a high roller on a losing streak. She's the last of an old family, and she is spending its money down on purpose, with grace, rather than leave it to a nephew's lawyers. The lakefront land is the one thing she's holding back, for someone who asks for it the right way. (No tie to the founder: Mike, 2026-10-28.)

## Current state
Vivian: early 60s, Black old money; her great-grandfather's insurance company covered the mill and dock workers; "a high roller on a losing streak"; her family owns the lakefront land; "her losses push her to sell, so how you treat her decides whether you or Tae gets it"; voice: "never says she's selling; lies with grace"; tell: pearls in her fist (people/vivian.md). Task 7's unsettled ideas include "Vivian's grandfather promised the founder lakefront land on a handshake" (plan/t07.md). The founder is offstage with no plot role (pages/walkons.md). Nothing explains why she's losing.

## Proposed change
**Why she's losing.** She isn't on a losing streak. She is spending the family down on purpose, with grace, at the tables, because she is the last of it, and her heir is a nephew in another state, and his lawyers, none of whom has seen the lake. The land is the one thing she won't let them have. She has been waiting for the right person to ask for it. Tae has asked, with a cheque. Nobody has asked her what it should become.

**How it shows:**
- **The yacht.** From the deck she looks at the Levee and Backwater, not the Mile: the part of the city her family's money came from. One insert, no caption.
- **The pearls.** Her copies-of-pearls reveal (already settled) gets a reason: the real ones went first, and she keeps the fist-tell because it's the only thing about her that still tells the truth.
- **Her voice drops one word.** "A rough season" is what she calls fifty years.
- **The ask (A5.1).** The land scene keeps the existing standing-based pricing. It gains one hidden option: before any price is named, the player can ask her what she'd want the land to be. If they do, she lowers the price one step and tells them. Nothing in the UI flags it. A player who leads with a number gets the number.

**Want:** to lose with dignity. **Need:** to be asked for the thing she's been holding.

## Why it works
It gives the game's most expensive decision a person behind it. "High roller on a losing streak" becomes a woman settling an inheritance, which puts her inside the story instead of beside it as a bank. It also makes Tae's bid and the player's bid differ in kind, not price, which is what the land contest needs.

## How the player experiences it
The yacht insert; the pearls; the A5.1 question that lowers the price.

## Risks and tradeoffs
The A5.1 question must not become the only good route: standing-based pricing stays the main path, and the question is a discount a thoughtful player stumbles into. The handshake with the founder from the first draft (and from plan/t07.md's unsettled ideas) is dropped.

- Mike, 2026-10-28: accepted without the handshake. There is no tie to the founder and no Gus discount. Kept: spending down on purpose, the heir, and holding the land for whoever asks the right way.
