## Summary
Build Vivian's secret from an idea already in the author's own notes: her grandfather promised Mr. Hamilton the lakefront parcel for the "modern future" Starflare, on a handshake, and the family reneged. She has known since she was a girl. The land is not an asset she's reluctant to sell; it's a debt her family never paid.

## Current state
Vivian: early 60s, Black old money; her great-grandfather's insurance company covered the mill and dock workers; "a high roller on a losing streak"; her family owns the lakefront land; "her losses push her to sell, so how you treat her decides whether you or Tae gets it"; voice: "never says she's selling; lies with grace"; tell: pearls in her fist (people/vivian.md). Task 7's unsettled ideas include "Vivian's grandfather promised the founder lakefront land on a handshake" (plan/t07.md). The founder is offstage with no plot role (pages/walkons.md). Nothing explains why she's losing.

## Proposed change
**The fact.** In the rocket age, Mr. Hamilton and Vivian's grandfather shook on it over a late table at the supper club: when the Flare outgrew the Levee, the Starflare's future would be built on the family's lakefront parcel at the Silvershore line, at a friend's price. The mills closed; the family's insurance fortune wobbled; the grandfather's sons quietly let the promise lapse and leased the parcel to no one. Hamilton never asked. Vivian, fourteen, was in the room for the handshake, because she was the one her grandfather brought to tables.

**Why she's losing.** She isn't on a losing streak. She is spending the family down on purpose, with grace, at the tables where it was made, because she is the last of it and her heir is a nephew in another state, and his lawyers, none of whom has seen the lake. The land is the one thing she won't let them have, and she has been waiting, for years, for the right person to ask for it. Tae has asked, with a cheque. Nobody has asked the way it was promised.

**How it shows:**
- **The sunglasses** are so nobody sees where she looks. From the yacht, she looks at the Levee, not the Mile. One insert, no caption.
- **The pearls.** Her copies-of-pearls reveal (already settled) gets a reason: the real ones went first, and she keeps the fist-tell because it's the only thing about her that still tells the truth.
- **Her voice drops one word.** "A rough season" is what she calls fifty years.
- **The ask (A5.1).** The land scene has a hidden variant: if the player has S4 (asked Gus about the band photo) and comes to her with Gus, Gus can say his father's name and hers in one sentence, and the price is the friend's price. Nothing in the UI says this; the player who brought Gus will feel the room change. Without it, the existing standing-based pricing applies.

**Want:** to lose with dignity. **Need:** to be asked for the thing she's been holding.

## Why it works
It takes a line the author already wrote down and makes it the spine of the game's most expensive decision. "High roller on a losing streak" becomes a woman settling an inheritance, which puts her inside the story instead of beside it as a bank. It also makes Tae's bid and the player's bid differ in kind, not price, which is what the land contest needs.

## How the player experiences it
The yacht insert; the pearls; the A5.1 variant with Gus in the room; one line at the Gilded Pine where she mentions "a man who used to bring his boy to the tables" and changes the subject (she lies with grace; the boy was Gus).

## Risks and tradeoffs
Gives the founder one offstage act (a handshake), which brushes "no plot role"; it's a memory, never a scene. The A5.1 variant must not become the only good route: keep standing-based pricing as the main path, and treat the handshake as a discount the right player stumbles into.
