## Summary
Make A4.2 playable: to help Gus quit the Cinder Room, you bring him to Richard's picnic table at the Slack Water and Richard plays Gus a pass of War for nothing, then plays you. The Cinder Room's house game is casino war; Richard's game is War. Gus is addicted to the paid version of the game an old man plays for free, and the old man is the one whose house Gus's casino took.

## Current state
A4.2 "Gus at the bottom: Cinder Room; help him quit, with Dot and Stella; leads to Gus's trust; Gus lets go (6)" (pages/storymap-beats.md). The Cinder Room's house game is casino war (plan/t03.md). Richard plays one-pass War at the picnic table (people/richard.md: "War's an honest game. No choices, no bluffing. Just the cards."). Richard and Gus have no scene together anywhere in the docs. Gus "relapses" in act 4 and borrows from Ray again (plan/t13.md).

## Proposed change
A4.2 becomes a short sequence:
1. **The bottom.** As written: you find him under the Stacks, out of chips, Ray's man at his elbow (if you took the collect-for-Ray job, the man is you).
2. **Over the water.** You drive him across the Long Bridge before dawn (the dead zone; S11 may fire). Dot comes out from behind the desk, the first time in the game (S-016), and says the time (S-012) or nothing. Stella comes in the morning if you told her (S-046).
3. **The honest game.** Richard is at the picnic table. They know each other: Gus gave him the key; Richard believes Gus pays his room. Neither says so. Gus sits. Richard deals one pass, 10 cards each, the existing two-player story-event game, and then deals you one. There is no money and no choice, which is the point: Gus has to sit through a game he can't lose anything at, across from the man his floor hurt most. The scene's stake is whether you say out loud what both of them are not saying (the house, the key, the room): say it and Richard's trust drops and Gus's rises; keep quiet and the reverse. Richard's line either way: "Cards don't care."
4. **The ask.** Afterward, Gus asks you for one thing: hold his Cinder Room cash until he asks for it back. He never asks. It sits in the story-key row, and tapping it reads "Gus gave you this to hold. He hasn't asked for it back." (S-009).

Signature moment: the insert of three hands at one picnic table, the rocket sign across the river in the plate, and Richard, who doesn't know, saying "Your friend plays like a man who's been told the rules."

Sample dialogue (Gus, to the voice sheet):
- "My dad used to say war was the only game you couldn't be bad at. He meant it as a compliment. Anyway."
- "No. I'll sit. You deal."
- "Dot. ... I know what time it is."
- On the drive back, the only time he says it plainly: "I owe people. That's the whole of it."

## Why it works
"Help him quit" is currently a line in a table; this gives it a mechanic the player already knows (the War mini-game) and a scene that only this game could write, because it comes from the game's own venue list: the mob's house game and an old man's free game are the same cards. It joins three threads (Gus, Richard, Dot) in the dead zone (S-004) and puts Gus across a table from the man his floor hurt most, without a speech. The held cash is loss aversion in the player's pocket for the rest of the game.

## How the player experiences it
Four scenes over one in-game night and morning: Cinder Room, Long Bridge, motel lot, picnic table. The War game runs as the existing story-event module. If the player never earned Richard's trust, the table is empty and Gus plays you instead, which is quieter and still works.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Depends on the War module existing (it is already specified). The Gus/Richard meeting risks pre-empting M4's room 9 climax if the player hasn't solved it; the two men's silence about the key keeps M4 intact, and the player's choice to break it is what feeds Richard's trust either way. If S-012 is rejected, Dot's line becomes a silent pose swap.
