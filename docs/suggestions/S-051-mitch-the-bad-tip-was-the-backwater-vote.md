## Summary
Specify Mitch's ruin: Bennett's "inside information" was that Backwater would vote yes on gaming. Mitch bought a Backwater storefront to open a slot parlor the day the licenses came. Backwater voted no. He has worked a gas station between the dry side and the wet side ever since, selling the only legal gamble in the Bridges to people crossing to the real ones.

## Current state
Mitch: "Years ago he made big bets on supposedly inside information and lost almost everything. The bad tip came from Bennett. It left him distrustful of everyone" (people/mitch.md). What the bet was is not stated. Backwater "voted no" on gaming; the Bridges got "a few slot licenses" (plan/t02.md). Mitch recognizes Bennett's lawyer and can name the trust account (A4.11).

## Proposed change
**The tip.** About thirty years ago, when the districts voted on licenses, Bennett, barely twenty and already running errands for the ward office, told a dozen Levee and Backwater people the fix was in: Backwater would go yes, and anyone holding a storefront on the main drag would have a slot license by spring. Several of them bought. Mitch, newly married and about the same age, bought biggest: a corner building on Backwater's main street with his savings and most of his father-in-law's. Backwater's mill families voted no. Bennett's friends, who'd been selling the storefronts, made money; Bennett got a cut, or a favor, or just the lesson that a tip is a product. Mitch lost the building, the marriage and the savings, and, years later, took the one job on the Bridges that was exactly halfway between the vote he lost and the casinos he can't afford.

**Why it matters now.** The lawyer on the Flare's filing is the same lawyer who handled the storefront sales. That's why Mitch recognizes him (already settled) and why the trust account he names (A4.11) is one he has a personal reason to remember: his money went through it once. The by-the-book route is Mitch's revenge served as a subpoena.

**How it shows:**
- **"Pump four's broken. Has been since I started."** The pump has a Backwater main-street address on its old service sticker. An insert, for the one player who looks.
- **The Haybale book** exists because a man who lost everything on one bet now sells small ones he can price himself.
- **Earning his trust** (already settled as finding out about Bennett) gets a scene: you say the name, he says nothing, and the next day the back room is open.
- **If Bennett loses the mayor's race** (S-039), Mitch's epilogue panel is him at Haybale Downs, with a program, betting a horse for the first time in twenty years.

## Why it works
It ties the cynic at the gas station to the city's founding fact (the dry vote) and to the game's antagonist, which the docs already half-do, and it makes his help in act 4 personal. A storefront lost to a vote is a house lost to the Levee, which puts Mitch in the game's theme (S-054) from the Bridges.

## How the player experiences it
One insert, one scene, one epilogue panel. He never tells the story straight; Dot or Kasia can tell a version of it.

## Risks and tradeoffs
"About thirty years ago" fits the bible's timeline (the licensing votes came with the land casinos, when Pete, hired 41 years ago, became one of the Flare's first dealers) and makes Bennett and Mitch about twenty at the time (and Kasia fifteen, S-025); check it against whatever date Mike settles for the vote (OQ 43). The father-in-law and marriage are new flavour.
