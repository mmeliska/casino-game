## Summary
The epilogue picks five or six cast panels by standing and flags. Give each panel's single insert a rule: it shows the character's object (mug, pin, vest, register, ledger, key, sunglasses, watch) in the state the player's choices left it. The object says what happened; the line says how they feel about it.

## Current state
Epilogue: about 8 panels; panels 2 to 7 are "the 5 or 6 cast you touched most, picked from standing, flags, heat and the scales," each "a still pose plus a line or two" (plan/t10.md). No rule for what the panel shows beyond the pose.

## Why it works
The epilogue builder is the one place the game reads back every choice at once; an object rule turns it from "here's who you knew" into "here's what you did to them," with no narration. It uses only inserts, which are the cheapest art unit.

## How the player experiences it
Eight panels at the end, each with an insert they recognize.

## Risks and tradeoffs
About three insert states per character is a small art list (most already exist as clue or seed inserts). The table depends on several other suggestions for its states; where one is rejected, the object still works with fewer states.
