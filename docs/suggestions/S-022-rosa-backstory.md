## Summary
A 350-word history of Rosa, consistent with the bible, written to give her lessons a source.

## Current state
Puerto Rican; her parents came up for factory work; grew up in the Levee; early 40s; a dealer's vest with the Starflare patch torn off; blacklisted for dealing Gus back to even; tied to the dealers' union; teaches the player; never enters the Flare (people/rosa.md).

## Proposed change
Rosa's mother ran a sewing machine at the uniform plant on the Levee's south end and her father fed a furnace; both came up from Ponce in the last decade the mills were hiring. She grew up two streets from the Stacks and learned cards from the men on her father's stoop, who played for cigarettes and taught her that a cheat's hands are quieter than an honest man's. She could deal a clean game at twelve and spot a dirty one at fourteen.

The Flare hired her at twenty-two, when it was already fading, because Pete watched her shuffle at a dealer-school audition and said nothing, which from Pete is a recommendation. She dealt blackjack on the Flare's floor for fifteen years and organized the dealers' local in its break room. She was the best pair of hands in the Levee and everyone who mattered knew it, which is why the payroll night was never a question of skill. It was a question of what to do when the owner, a kind man who couldn't look at her, sat down drunk at her table with the staff's money and started losing it.

She dealt him back to even. It took forty minutes and she did it so cleanly that the overhead camera saw only a dealer's hands and a strange run of cards. When the commission came she said nothing, because saying something meant saying his name, and because the men on the stoop had taught her that the hand that isn't busy doesn't talk. She tore the patch off the vest the day the letter came and kept wearing the vest, which is the most Rosa thing about her: she won't throw away what she's lost, and she won't pretend it's still hers.

She deals Kasia's back room for cash and the union pays her dues. She stands at the Prairie Queen landing because the river is the closest she'll get to a licensed floor. She has not seen Gus since. She does not hate him; she has put him where she puts everything she can't fix, behind the rules, and she teaches the rules to anyone who'll listen, because teaching is the one way to keep her hands in the game. When she tells you faces are for amateurs, she is telling you how she survived.

## Why it works
It roots the voice sheet (rules, odds, the hand that isn't busy) in a childhood and a choice, and it explains the torn-patch vest as a character trait rather than a prop. It also makes clear she and Gus have never discussed it, which S-021 needs.

## How the player experiences it
Through the lessons (S-023), the vest, the landing, and the union pin on Kasia's collar that she looks at once.

## Risks and tradeoffs
New flavour facts: Ponce, the uniform plant, the stoop. The dealer-school audition with Pete adds a small Pete-Rosa origin the docs don't have; it fits "Pete goes quiet at her name."
