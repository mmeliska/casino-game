## Summary
Give Stella a stated want (to keep her father alive) and need (to let him be what he is), fix the order in which she learns he gambles (the bible flags that she "doesn't know" yet helps him quit at A4.2), and give her one signature moment: on opening night she runs the numbers and doesn't say them, not because it hurts (the last night, S-003) but because it's enough.

## Current state
Stella: mid-20s, finance analyst, has run the Flare's numbers; wants Gus to sell for his health; never sees the Ray debt; noticed the remortgage; "She doesn't know he gambles, so the torn patch leaves her speechless"; voice: figures for the Flare, stories for Dad; A4.2 "help him quit, with Dot and Stella" (people/stella.md, pages/storymap-beats.md). The bible's OQ 33 notes the order problem.

## Proposed change
**Want:** to keep him. **Need:** to stop running him nine ways.

**When she learns (OQ 33, settled by Mike 2026-10-27).** She learns from you, if you choose to tell her. After Gus's confession at A4.1 (S-004 places it at Dot's), the player's forgive-or-hold choice gets a third stake: "Tell Stella." If you tell her, A4.2 opens with her already there, colder, and "help him quit, with Dot and Stella" is literal; the torn patch then leaves her speechless for a different reason: not that he gambles, but that someone else paid for it years ago and he let them. If you don't tell her, she doesn't know: A4.2 runs with Dot alone, and she learns from the torn patch, speechless, exactly as her sheet says. Gus never tells her himself; he can't finish the sentence.

**Arc stages:**
1. **The nine ways** (acts 1 and 2): figures, worry as health, the meetings with Tae, the false lead.
2. **The remortgage** (act 2 or 3): she tells you about it as proof he's propping up the Flare. She's wrong, and the player may know it.
3. **The morning after** (A4.1/A4.2, only if you told her): she comes to Dot's. Her line, to the voice sheet: "Dad. ... How much." He gives her the number; she already knows the rest from you. She doesn't write it down.
4. **The hearing** (A5.7): add her to the people who can speak for you (the settled list is Rosa, Pete, Vivian's lawyer, Ed and Graham/Claire). If she does, she speaks in stories, not figures, for the first time. She speaks for you only if she heard about the gambling from you and not from the patch.

**Signature moment.** Opening night, O1 (Gus retired and a guest; S-001). She's at the new cage with the first night's drop in front of her. Insert: the count sheet, blank. Pose: the analyst pose, then a swap. She closes the ledger without writing the total. To Gus: "It's enough." That's the only number she says all night.

**Badly (O6, Gus lets go):** the resting point's "visits with sober Gus, Dot and Stella" are at the picnic table, and she brings a deck, not a laptop. That ending is hers more than anyone's; it should feel earned, not like a consolation.

## Why it works
The bible's ordering gap is a gift: making the telling the player's choice gives Stella her one scene with her father that isn't about the Flare, and makes keeping quiet a thing the player did. The unsaid total is the analyst's version of the game's rule that the key line is the one nobody says.

## How the player experiences it
A third option at A4.1; a short morning scene; the hearing in stories; the blank count sheet. No new art beyond one Stella pose.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Changes A4.1's choice from two options to three; keeps the two existing effects. A player who never tells her gets the docs' version (speechless at the patch) and loses her as a hearing witness. If S-004 is rejected, the morning scene happens in Gus's office. O6's picnic-table version depends on nothing else.
