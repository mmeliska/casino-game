## Summary
Give Stella a stated want (to keep her father alive) and need (to let him be what he is), fix the order in which she learns he gambles (the bible flags that she "doesn't know" yet helps him quit at A4.2), and give her one signature moment: on opening night she runs the numbers and doesn't say them, not because it hurts (the last night, S-003) but because it's enough.

## Current state
Stella: mid-20s, finance analyst, has run the Flare's numbers; wants Gus to sell for his health; never sees the Ray debt; noticed the remortgage; "She doesn't know he gambles, so the torn patch leaves her speechless"; voice: figures for the Flare, stories for Dad; A4.2 "help him quit, with Dot and Stella" (people/stella.md, pages/storymap-beats.md). The bible's OQ 33 notes the order problem.

## Proposed change
**Want:** to keep him. **Need:** to stop running him nine ways.

**When she learns (resolves OQ 33).** She learns from Gus, not from the patch, at A4.1: after his confession to the player (S-004 places it at Dot's), the player's forgive-or-hold choice gets a third stake: "Tell Stella, or let him." If the player lets him, Gus tells her himself the next morning, in the one scene where he finishes his sentences (S-011's turn can be this). If the player tells her, A4.2 opens with her already there and colder. The torn patch then leaves her speechless for a different reason: not that he gambles, but that someone else paid for it years ago and he let them.

**Arc stages:**
1. **The nine ways** (acts 1 and 2): figures, worry as health, the meetings with Tae, the false lead.
2. **The remortgage** (act 2 or 3): she tells you about it as proof he's propping up the Flare. She's wrong, and the player may know it.
3. **The morning after** (A4.1/A4.2): she learns. Her line, to the voice sheet: "Dad. ... How much." He tells her. She doesn't write it down.
4. **The hearing** (A5.7): add her to the people who can speak for you (the settled list is Rosa, Pete, Vivian's lawyer, Ed and Graham/Claire). If she does, she speaks in stories, not figures, for the first time.

**Signature moment.** Opening night, O1. She's at the new cage with the first night's drop in front of her. Insert: the count sheet, blank. Pose: the analyst pose, then a swap. She closes the ledger without writing the total. To Gus: "It's enough." That's the only number she says all night.

**Badly (O6, Gus lets go):** the resting point's "visits with sober Gus, Dot and Stella" are at the picnic table, and she brings a deck, not a laptop. That ending is hers more than anyone's; it should feel earned, not like a consolation.

## Why it works
The bible's ordering gap is a gift: making Gus tell her, as a choice the player grants or takes, gives Stella her one scene with her father that isn't about the Flare. The unsaid total is the analyst's version of the game's rule that the key line is the one nobody says.

## How the player experiences it
A third option at A4.1; a short morning scene; the hearing in stories; the blank count sheet. No new art beyond one Stella pose.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Changes A4.1's choice from two options to three; keeps the two existing effects. If S-004 is rejected, the morning scene happens in Gus's office. O6's picnic-table version depends on nothing else.
