## Summary
A 300-word history that fits both versions, and five lines that track the voice from friendly to clipped to, once, honest.

## Current state
Both versions: white, 30s, upper-middle-income family, well dressed but not over the top at first, divorced, no kids, starting fresh, a head start (people/chase.md). Voice settled. No history.

## Proposed change
**Backstory.** They grew up in a suburb where nobody gambled and everybody bet: on houses, on schools, on which car said what. They married at twenty-six someone who was better at the betting than they were, bought a house that was a stretch, and spent ten years keeping up. They were good at it, which is the problem; they were good at a game whose rules they never agreed to. The marriage ended the way those do, without a villain, and the house went to the one who'd put more in. They took the settlement, the car and the watch their father gave them when they got the first job, and drove until the money said stop. Northlight said stop. They have told the story of why they came a dozen ways at a dozen tables, and the only true version is the one they tell nobody: they are here because it's the first city they ever saw where you could lose in public and come back the next night.

They are, in fact, a good player. Careful, patient, the kind who leaves a table up. They have never once taken a job in Northlight. They think the player is a little reckless and, early on, a little charming, and the friendliness in act 1 is real: they'd like a friend who doesn't know what they lost. The arrogance that follows is a run of wins said in the voice of someone who needs them to be deserved. The fall is variance and then Ray, and the ask at A3.8 is the first honest sentence they've said since the suburb.

**Sample dialogue.**
- Act 1, friendly: "First night? Me too, more or less. Crowd thins out after eleven. Easier to get a seat. Don't tell anyone I told you."
- Act 2, showy: "Just a cold night. I'll be up by Friday."
- Act 2, the tell: (checks the watch) "This? Nothing. Habit."
- Act 3, the ask: "I'm not up. I haven't been up since spring. I need two thousand by Friday and I don't have a Friday."
- Act 4, all is well: "You know what the difference between us was? You picked a side of the river. I just kept crossing."

## Why it works
The backstory keeps every settled fact and gives the voice sheet a reason: "their wins, never their losses" is a suburb's rule. The dialogue shows the arc in five lines, with the Friday line paying off their own act 2 line.

## How the player experiences it
Through lines only; the history is never told.
