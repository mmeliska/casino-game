## Summary
Define the license hearing's "one hard question" (A5.7): the commission asks whether Gus has ever played in an unlicensed game. You can tell the truth, dress it up, or claim the Cinder Room seat as your own. The third answer is Rosa's choice, made again by the player, with Rosa in the room.

## Current state
A5.7 (pages/storymap-beats.md): "answer one hard question honestly or dress it up; Bennett's stance set by your act 4 route; license restored, or restored with conditions (never lost)." The question itself is not established. M1 (plan/t08.md): Gus, drunk, lost the payroll at Rosa's table; she dealt him back to even and took the fall; the commission only ever saw the overhead dealer cam; Gus kept the only wide-angle tape, which Bennett holds after A3.5. Gus's Cinder Room gambling is known to Wren's cameras, seen by the player at A1.9 (and held as the S6 IOU), and admitted by Gus at A4.1; the commission has never been shown it (people/gus.md, people/wren.md). The writing rule "bluffs run on information" (pages/writing.md): a lie works only if nobody present holds a contradicting fact.

## Proposed change
The commissioner's question, asked after your witnesses speak: "The application names Gus as host of this floor. Has he ever sat in an unlicensed game while holding a license in this state?" Gus is at the table beside you (the docs don't place him at the hearing; this suggestion does). Three actions, none labelled:

- **Answer plainly.** The truth. Gus goes off the license as an owner-host (he can still cut the ribbon as a guest, so the O1/O2 choice at A5.6 is untouched), the new Starflare opens with a condition (a season of commission audits), and Ed and Graham/Claire count it for you; Stella, if she's in the room, hears it. Gus looks at the mug.
- **Dress it up.** "Not that I've ever seen." A bluff on information. It holds if nobody in the room can contradict it: Bennett stays silent if your act 4 route was reconcile or by-the-book, and Wren's footage never reached evidence. It fails if you exposed Umbra at A4.3 (her servers are in evidence) or Bennett is hostile (he still has the wide-angle tape). A failed bluff is the strictest set of conditions in the game, never a lost license.
- **Take the seat.** "The seat was mine. He drove me home." You put the unlicensed play on your own record. Conditions land on you (a fine and a few weeks of licensed-only play before opening night). Gus's record stays clean. If Rosa is one of your witnesses, she is the one person in the room who knows exactly what you just did, and her pose swaps once. Gus never thanks you for it out loud; later, on the new floor, he says her name for the first time in years (see S-021).

Sample panel, black: *Nobody at the table looked at Gus. That was the tell.*

## Why it works
The game's founding secret is a woman taking the fall for Gus at a table. Putting the same choice in the player's hands at the climax is mirroring and shared hardship at once: the player either repeats Rosa's sacrifice knowing what it cost her, tells the truth she couldn't, or lies the way Gus has lied all game. It turns a procedural beat into the thematic one, and it spends existing assets (Bennett's tape, Wren's footage, the act 4 routes) as the information that decides whether a bluff holds. It also gives "never fails" teeth: the worst case is a cost you chose to carry.

## How the player experiences it
Hearing-room scene after the witnesses. Two-shot of you and Gus; pose swap on Gus (chin-up to mug) when the question lands; Rosa's one finger on the table if she's present; a black panel before the answer. The three options are actions with a stake shown ("Take the seat. (A fine, and clean play until opening night)"). Conditions appear afterwards in the Wallet as a line item, like any other debt.

## Risks and tradeoffs
It makes Gus's gambling the climax's hinge, which the docs already imply but never stage; players who skipped M1 and A4.2 will meet the fact late, so the question needs one line of setup from the commissioner. The bluff branch needs four states (Bennett friendly/hostile, Wren exposed/not), all of which already exist as flags. If Gus is absent or has turned on you (the O2 track), the question is asked about you instead (your own unlicensed play) and only the first two answers exist. Conflicts with S-002, which makes Gus the one who speaks; pick one shape for the hearing.
