## Summary
Pay off Richard's line "My boy used to peek at the top card. I let him win anyway." In the room 9 reconcile scene, Bennett peeks, Richard sees it, says nothing, and loses the hand on purpose. Only then does he hand over the box.

## Current state
Richard's voice sheet (people/richard.md): "War's an honest game. No choices, no bluffing. Just the cards." and "My boy used to peek at the top card. I let him win anyway." The reconcile outcome (people/richard.md): "give Bennett the box and reconcile father and son (they play a hand of War; out of gratitude Bennett files the same amendment)"; pages/room9.md's version has them "talk for the first time in years" without the hand. Nothing is written for the hand itself.

## Proposed change
The reconcile scene is played, not told, as a one-pass game of War at the picnic table (the existing story-event game), with you watching from the motel steps. Panels:

1. Plate: the picnic table, late afternoon, the rocket sign across the river.
2. Insert: Bennett's thumb lifting the corner of the top card. No caption.
3. Pose swap: Richard, eyes on the cards, then on his son. No line.
4. The hand plays out. Richard loses by one card.
5. Richard slides the box across. "You always did."
6. Black panel: *Bennett didn't ask what he meant.*

Shame route variant: Richard catches the peek and names it ("You still peek") before he makes the threat; the grudge hardens because the father has finally said the thing out loud. Burn route: Richard shuffles the deck back together and the cards are the last panel.

## Why it works
Setup and payoff through a prop and a gesture, with no exposition, which is the panel kit working as designed. It says everything about Bennett (a man who has peeked at the top card his whole career) and about Richard (a father who let him win and never said so) in one insert. It also makes the gentlest route the most memorable one, which nudges players toward the ending that costs Gus the least.

## How the player experiences it
A short story-event game the player watches rather than plays, which is a first for the game and marks the scene as different. The peek insert is the only new art.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Players who never played War with Richard will miss the line it pays off; the scene still works as a silent father-and-son hand. Risk of being too neat: keep Richard's line to three words and let the black panel do the rest.

## Decision notes
- Mike, 2026-10-27: rejected. The reconcile ending stays as the docs have it (a hand of War, the box, the amendment), with no peek and no thrown hand.
