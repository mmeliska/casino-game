## Summary
A 350-word history for Dot, and a reframing of her third story: the Hawaiian shirt is a souvenir of the one trip she took after her big win, and the person she took was her sister. The shirt is the one her sister picked out for her.

## Current state
Dot's stories (people/dot.md): the flood (she let everyone sleep upstairs free; the dockworkers brought food), the man in room 9, and "once she trusts you, the shirt (a souvenir of the one trip she took after her own big win)." Her sister was Gus's wife; "That is a reveal (a framed photo of the two sisters in Gus's office), never part of her intro."

## Proposed change
**Backstory.** Dot and her sister grew up in a Backwater two-flat; their father ran the hoist at the Backwater grain elevator and bowled Tuesdays at Millstone Lanes before it was Millstone Lanes. Dot was the older one, the practical one, the one who stayed. Her sister was the one who went over the water. Dot bought the Slack Water with a settlement after the elevator accident that killed their father, and ran it the way he ran his crew: everyone fed, everyone's business their own. She was never a gambler; she played the state lottery once a year on his birthday, and one year it hit. Not life-changing money. Enough for a trip. She took her sister, because Gus wouldn't leave the Flare for a week and Alice hadn't been out of Northlight since the wedding. They went somewhere warm for nine days. Alice bought two shirts, loud ones, and made Dot wear hers on the plane home. The flood came the next spring; the room 9 envelopes came years after that; the phone call came in between. Dot has worn the shirt every day since the funeral. It isn't a souvenir of the win. It's the last thing her sister picked out.

**The story, as she tells it.** Unchanged until the last line, told in 3 or 4 old-photo panels per the writing rules: two women on a beach, one in each shirt. Then, back at the desk, the line she adds only once she trusts you: "She had better taste than me. I got the loud one." The player who has seen the photo in Gus's office knows who "she" is. Dot never says so.

**One more use.** Opening night (S-016): Dot crosses the river in the shirt. Gus has seen it once, at dawn on her lot (A4.2, S-015), and didn't take it in; under the new floor's lights he knows which trip it's from. Pose swap, no line.

## Why it works
The shirt is already her most distinctive prop and already gated behind trust; this makes it a wound she wears instead of a quirk. It turns the "big win" story into the game's thesis in miniature: money bought nine days with someone, and that's all money ever buys here. It also gives the Dot-Gus silence a physical object that Gus has to look at on the last night.

## How the player experiences it
The old-photo story, the one added line, the shirt on opening night. Never a caption.

## Risks and tradeoffs
The elevator accident adds a fact (the sisters' father) the docs don't have; it's flavour, and the docs ask for specific, non-cliché family jobs. Depends on S-013 only for the name; without it, "she" still works.
