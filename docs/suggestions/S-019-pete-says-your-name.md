## Summary
Make Pete's "calls everyone kid until they earn their name" a gameplay moment the player can see coming: the Flare's rating card. When it shows a rating, Pete uses your name, once, and it's the name from Dot's register. That is the act 1 emotional beat: you belong somewhere.

## Current state
Pete "calls everyone 'kid' until they earn their name" and holds "a rating card in hand" (people/pete.md); his sample line: "Kid. Your rating says you're a $50 player. Play like one." The name is entered at A1.1 and "the game uses it from then on" (pages/player.md). A1.7 "Becoming a regular" has the choice "keep Gus's secret or not" and leads to "meet Pete and Jalen." The review proposed Pete's card show "what the Flare thinks you're worth" (pages/review.md). Comps tied to the card are a build note, not a scene.

## Proposed change
- **The card is a visible thing.** After A1.7 the Flare's floor has a small inset the player can tap: Pete's rating card for you. It's blank at first ("kid"). Each Flare sitting fills a line (average bet, hours). It's the only place in the game where the player sees a number about themselves, and it isn't theirs; it's the house's.
- **The moment.** The first time the card carries a rating (a few sittings, tuned), the next break point at the Flare fires a four-panel scene: Pete at the pit, insert of the card with a number, pose unchanged, and the line, with your register name in it: "[Name]. Your rating says you're a $50 player. Play like one." Nothing else changes. Comps start the next visit.
- **The rule afterward.** Pete uses your name from then on, and goes back to "kid" exactly once: the dark pit (A3.4), when he hands you the pin. If you sold Gus out, he never says your name again.
- **Jalen notices.** If Jalen is on the floor he says, politely, "He doesn't do that." That's the only comment.

## Why it works
Belonging is the first thing a blank-slate player needs and the hardest to give without a speech. The name is the one thing the player made; hearing it from the man who withholds names turns a settings field into a reward (technique: earned recognition, ritual). It also makes the comp system the leash the review asked for, because the card is the first thing Tae's Aurelian will "back off."

## How the player experiences it
A tappable card inset on the Flare's floor, one short scene, and a change in address that the player will hear in every Pete line afterward. In the drive-away epilogue, Pete's line is just your name (S-010).

## Risks and tradeoffs
Showing a number violates "the scales are never shown as numbers" only in spirit: the rating is the house's opinion of your bet size, not a hidden scale, and the docs already give Pete a rating line. The reversion to "kid" at the dark pit should be a pose-less, single-word choice by the writer; if it reads as cruelty, drop it and keep the name.
