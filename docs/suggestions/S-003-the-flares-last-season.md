## Summary
Between the Starflare Field drawings (A3.1; the announcement itself is A2.8) and the condemnation (A3.3), give the Flare a visible last season and a set of one-minute "last night" scenes, one per Flare person. The last night is one in-game night with three part-of-day slots, so the player can spend it with at most three of six people. The ones you didn't choose are the ones you remember.

## Current state
Act 3 is "the fall, always happens" (pages/gdd.md). A3.4 gives Pete a last-night scene at the dark pit; Miles/Audrey's test (beat 4) is set on "their last night at the dark Flare" (people/miles.md); S3's trucker saves the spaceman on the last night (plan/t09.md). The decline is shown through plate states (pages/writing.md: "more lights out, a roped-off table, the piano covered"). Nothing else marks the Flare's end for Gus, Jalen or Stella, and the player has no choice about how to spend the last night.

## Proposed change
1. **The last season plate.** After A3.1, the Flare plate gets a final state: the stadium drawing taped to the cage glass, the Rocket Wheel roped off, a FOR THE LEVEE banner Bennett's people hung on the sign. Bets stay the same; the floor is emptier.
2. **The last night.** A3.3's "last night on the floor" becomes a night with three slots, using the parts of the day the clock already has (afternoon, night, before dawn). Six scenes are available; each takes one slot:
   - **Gus, the office.** He is boxing the framed photo of the two sisters and can't decide whether to take it. "She picked the piano. Did you know that? Anyway." (He never says her name; S-013.) Stake: offer to carry the box across the river to Dot's, or let him keep it.
   - **Pete, the dark pit** (A3.4 as written).
   - **Jalen, the poker room.** The regulars put up a no-money game and give him the dealer's seat he has watched for years. He deals like he's done it forever. Stake: sit in and let him read you.
   - **Stella, the cage.** She counts the drop down for the last time, out loud, and stops before the total. "You don't need the number." Stake: say it for her, or don't.
   - **Miles/Audrey, the piano** (their beat 4 as written).
   - **The spaceman** (S3's reveal if seeded; otherwise you watch the District's crew bolt a cover over him).
3. **What can't be missed.** Pete's pin is a main-line beat (A3.4, it hires him back at A5.3), so if you don't choose the pit, Pete finds you at the doors on your way out, shorter, and still hands you the pin. Miles/Audrey's sighting of the tape (the M1 clue) still reaches you later as their "friend" line if you skipped the piano. Scarcity costs you the scene, never the key or the clue.
4. **Who you missed.** At A3.5 each person you didn't spend a slot with gets one line later, somewhere else, about that night. Jalen: "The regulars let me deal. You'd have liked it." That line is the only reproach the game makes.

## Why it works
Loss needs a last time, and scarcity makes a last time hurt. Making the player choose three of six scenes (five people and a statue) turns the fall from an announcement into a night they spent, and the people they skipped become a small, permanent regret the epilogue can read back. It builds on scenes that already exist (Pete, the piano, the trucker) rather than replacing them.

## How the player experiences it
On the last night, entering the Flare offers the slots as a short menu of places on the floor (office, pit, poker room, cage, lounge, front doors). Each scene is 4 to 8 panels. The before-dawn slot ends on the Long Bridge crossing home, which is already a dead zone and a break point.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Four new short scenes, a fallback for Pete at the doors, plus six "missed you" lines is real writing cost. The three-slot limit will frustrate completionists; the counter is that the drive-away and resting-point epilogues can mention who you were with. The office scene depends on naming Gus's wife (S-013) only for its line; it works without it.
