## Summary
A 350-word history for Kasia, consistent with the bible and S-024.

## Current state
Born in Backwater to a mill family, Polish-American dad, Mexican-American mom; took over her dad's tavern where the mill shifts drank; knows everyone's family; warm but tough; gives credit with a ledger (people/kasia.md).

## Proposed change
Kasia's father was a businessman, not a mill man (Mike, 2026-10-28). He sold restaurant supply out of a van, and he saw what nobody else across the river had bothered to see. Three shifts a day came out of the rolling mill gate and walked past a block with no bar on it. He bought the corner building on credit, put in a long counter and a kitchen, and called it the Tap. Her mother ran the kitchen and the books, and the pierogi are her mother's recipe served in her father's bar, which Kasia thinks is the correct ratio for most things. She grew up on the stool at the end of the bar doing homework in the ledger's margins. She learned the town the way a bartender's kid does: who paid, who didn't, and who said what about whom after the third beer.

He rented the back room to the mill local for its meetings because a hall full of thirsty men was good business. Then the mills closed. The riverboats came, across the water, and the men who used to drink at the Tap after a shift started crossing the Long Bridge after a sailing instead. When the state opened the door to land casinos, every district voted, and Backwater's vote was counted in the Tap's back room. Kasia was fifteen. The local, what was left of it, voted with the rest of Backwater to keep the casinos out. Her father stood at the back and said it was the right thing. A casino on this side of the river would have emptied his bar in a year. Kasia was in the room, and she has always remembered it as a town standing up for itself. She has never once thought about the rent. The sign is still on the door.

Her father kept the bar open on credit until the credit ran out. Then he kept it open on the "for amusement only" machines his daughter talked him into, which paid out from the till and kept the lights on. He never played one. He died with the bar in her name and the ledger in her hand.

She has run it twenty years. The back room is a card game because a card game is what the room was built for, and she pays Rosa to deal it because Rosa is union and the room was the mill local's. That, Kasia tells herself, is the difference between the Tap and the Flare. She has never been across the river for pleasure. She extends credit because her father did, and she keeps the ledger because her mother did. She has never once forgiven a skipped debt, because the one time her father did, the bar nearly went under.

She knows about Gus the way Backwater knows about the Levee: as weather. She has never met him. She knows Dot's sister married him. She thinks a man who keeps a dead man's place open on debt is a fool, and she would not like to be asked how the Tap stays open.

## Why it works
It gives the ledger, the machines and the door one cause, and it sharpens S-024's need: the "principle" she keeps was her father's business, and it sets up the mirror with Gus without either of them knowing it. The last paragraph is the hinge S-024's stage 2 turns on.

## How the player experiences it
The sign on the back-room door; the ledger margins; one line about her mother's recipe; her silence about the river.
