## Summary
A 350-word history for Kasia, consistent with the bible and S-024.

## Current state
Born in Backwater to a mill family, Polish-American dad, Mexican-American mom; took over her dad's tavern where the mill shifts drank; knows everyone's family; warm but tough; gives credit with a ledger (people/kasia.md).

## Proposed change
Kasia's father worked the rolling mill across the river and bought the tavern at the end of the street with a loan from the local, so the shift would have somewhere to go that wasn't a company bar. Her mother ran the kitchen and the books, and the pierogi are her mother's recipe served in her father's bar, which Kasia thinks is the correct ratio for most things. She grew up on the stool at the end of the bar doing homework in the ledger's margins, and she learned the town the way a bartender's kid does: by who paid, who didn't, and who said what about whom after the third beer.

When the mills closed she was fifteen. The local held its last meeting in the back room and voted, with the rest of Backwater, to keep the casinos out; her father spoke for it, and the sign is still on the door. The riverboats came anyway, across the water, and the men who used to drink at the Tap after a shift started crossing the Long Bridge after a sailing instead. Her father kept the bar open on pride and credit until the credit ran out, and then on the "for amusement only" machines his daughter talked him into, which paid out from the till and kept the lights on. He never played one. He died with the pin on his collar and the bar in her name.

She has run it twenty years. The back room is a card game because a card game is what the room was built for, and she pays Rosa to deal it because Rosa is union and the room is union and that, Kasia tells herself, is the difference between the Tap and the Flare. She has never been across the river for pleasure. She extends credit because her father did, and she keeps the ledger because her mother did, and she has never once forgiven a skipped debt because the one time her father did, it was the local's loan, and it closed the mill's last tavern but one.

She knows about Gus the way Backwater knows about the Levee: as weather. She has never met him. She knows Dot's sister married him. She thinks a man who keeps a dead man's place open on debt is a fool, and she would not like to be asked how the Tap stays open.

## Why it works
It gives the ledger, the pin, the machines and the door one cause, and it sets up the mirror with Gus without either of them knowing it. The last paragraph is the hinge S-024's stage 2 turns on.

## How the player experiences it
The sign on the back-room door; the ledger margins; one line about her mother's recipe; her silence about the river.

## Risks and tradeoffs
New flavour: the local's loan, the last meeting, the mother's books. Keeps every settled fact.
