## Summary
Give Jalen a reason for his politeness and his rule about money: his older brother had Raffy's route before Raffy, took Ray's money once too often, and is in Lock Street doing a stretch. Jalen's "never asks for money outright" is a vow, and his courtesy is armor. Raffy knows. The player learns it from Raffy, not Jalen.

## Current state
Jalen: early 20s, grew up in Backwater, saving to get out of Backwater, learned poker by watching, "well spoken and super polite ... never complains about Backwater, never asks for money outright" (people/jalen.md). Lies by "politeness: acting like it doesn't matter to him." No family, wound or reason for the rule is established. Raffy "runs envelopes for Ray's collections" and grew up in Backwater (people/raffy.md). Lock Street Station is the vice squad's base and "Lock Street" is slang for its lockup (people/ed.md). The two Backwater kids have no stated link.

## Proposed change
The fact: Jalen's brother Marcus (name offered; first names only per the rule) is three years older and ran Ray's envelopes for two years. He borrowed against the route, couldn't cover it, and did what Ray's debtors do: a crooked job that went wrong. He's a year into a short sentence at the county jail across town (unnamed; not Lock Street, which is the vice squad's overnight lockup). Jalen visits on Sundays and has never told anyone at the Flare. The busboy job is the first money in the house that didn't come through Ray, and Jalen's rule, never ask, never owe, is a wall he built around his mother's rent.

How it shows:
- **Raffy says it.** The first time you see Raffy at Millstone Lanes after you've met Jalen (A1.7 comes after A1.5, so this is usually a later Lanes visit, or a run if S-050 is taken): "Jalen's brother had this route. Before me. Tell him I said hey. Don't tell him I'm on it." That's the whole reveal. Raffy inherited the job Jalen's family lost a son to, and knows it.
- **Jalen won't take dirty money.** If the player's crooked lean is high, Jalen declines the stake. Politely, once: "I appreciate it, sir. I'd rather not know where it's from." It's the only time in the game an NPC refuses the player's money on principle, and the player has to climb straight to stake him.
- **Sundays.** Jalen is never on the floor on a Sunday afternoon. Pete knows why and doesn't say.
- **S10's threat, sharpened.** Wren's threat against Jalen (for a spotless player) is specific: she can get his brother's time extended or shortened. That's what she has on him.
- **Lock Street.** If the player ever spends a night in the lockup (a raid at high heat), Jalen hears it from Pete. The next time you're on the floor he brings you a coffee you didn't order and says "sir" and nothing else. He knows what a night there means.

## Why it works
"The one the player should want to see succeed" needs a reason to be at risk, and a wound that lives in the same system (Ray's debt ladder) that the player is being tempted by. It ties Jalen to Raffy and to Lock Street, two things the player already touches, and it makes his refusal of dirty money a scene instead of a flag. (Technique: the likable character's secret cost; mirroring the player's crooked path with its victim.)

## How the player experiences it
Raffy's line; the stake refusal if you've gone crooked; the coffee after a night in lockup; Wren's specific threat. Jalen never tells the story himself.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Adds an offstage brother and a county jail (unnamed, never shown); both are flavour and never need a scene. The stake refusal must not lock the Jalen arc for crooked players: he accepts once you've played licensed for a season (heat cooling already models this). Check against the tone rule: Backwater stays a place people like; the brother's story is about Ray, not the district.
