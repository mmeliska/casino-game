## Summary
A 350-word history of Tae, written to make "the local who came home" and "the salesman who never lies" the same man, plus four lines.

## Current state
Korean-American, early 40s; grew up in the Levee; made it big with the out-of-town chain and asked to be sent home; his dad drummed in the Flare's house band; family friend of Gus's family, big brother to Stella; wants to buy Gus out fairly and own the name for a restaurant; opposes bulldozing the Flare; bids for Vivian's land; takes the Revaris cameras in act 2 (people/tae.md).

## Proposed change
Tae's parents came to the Levee a few years before the mills began to close, which turned out to be bad timing for a drummer and a seamstress, and the Flare's house band was the only steady gig in a city that was running out of them. He grew up in a second-floor flat on the Flare's block, doing homework to the band through the wall, and learned two things early: that the Flare was the warmest room in the Levee, and that it paid his father late. He hung around the floor as a kid because Gus let him, and he held Stella on his lap at the cage while the drop was counted, which is why she still trusts him with numbers.

He left for college on a scholarship and came back for his father's funeral with a chain's name on his business card and a plan for his city that he'd been drawing since the band folded. The chain sent him home because he asked, and because the Aurelian's numbers in a Great Lakes casino city were better than anyone expected, which was his pitch. He doesn't lie because he's never needed to: the city he wants is better than the one it has, and he's willing to say so to anyone, including the man whose supper club was the only thing that ever treated his father as a professional.

He believes Gus is killing himself for a building. He also believes the building is the best thing the Levee ever made, which is why he wants the name and not the lot. He has never reconciled the two, and he doesn't try; he builds. The satellite offer was sincere. The restaurant is sincere. The camera deal (S13) is the one decision he made because corporate told him to, and it is the one he'll regret. He bids for Vivian's land because the Aurelian needs a tower and because, if the Starflare is going to rise on the lake, a part of him wants it to be his.

**Sample dialogue.**
- "Northlight deserves a world-class house. Gus deserves to rest."
- "My father played here eleven years. They paid him late every one of them. He loved it. I'm not sure what that proves."
- On the cameras (S13 warning): "Corporate likes the price. I like the price. That's usually when something's wrong, isn't it."
- To Stella: "Your dad let me sit at the cage when I was eight. He told me not to count out loud. I counted out loud."

## Why it works
The history makes his vision and his soft spot the same wound, so he stays a rival without a villain, as settled. The "paid him late" line gives his rivalry a specific grievance that is also a love.
