## Summary
Define the license hearing's "one hard question" (A5.7): the commission asks whether Gus knew what he was signing when he put Umbra's cameras on a licensed floor, and when you found out. You can tell the truth, dress it up, or take the concealment onto your own record. The third answer is Rosa's choice, made again by the player, with Rosa in the room. Gus is never on the new Starflare's license: he retires, and in O1 he cuts the ribbon as a guest.

## Current state
A5.7 (pages/storymap-beats.md): "answer one hard question honestly or dress it up; Bennett's stance set by your act 4 route; license restored, or restored with conditions (never lost)." The question itself is not established, and the docs don't place Gus in the room. The hearing is a test of the player's record: the clean-or-dirty rule (pages/storymap-branches.md) says a black-hat act 4 route makes "the license hearing weigh it and set stricter conditions." M2 (people/gus.md, plan/t08.md): the commission ordered new cameras after the payroll night; Wren offered hers for a fraction of the cost; when Gus refused, she blackmailed him with Cinder Room footage; he signed knowing it was Umbra. Gus admits this to the player at A4.1. The writing rule "bluffs run on information" (pages/writing.md): a lie works only if nobody present holds a contradicting fact. O1 (A5.6) says only that Gus cuts the ribbon; nothing in the docs makes him an owner of the new Starflare, and Stella's whole thread is that he should stop.

## Proposed change
Gus sits beside you at the hearing as the man who signed the camera contract, called to attend, not to apply. After your witnesses have spoken, the commissioner turns to you:

"This commission ordered the cameras on that floor. We have since had reason to look at the company that installed them. The man who signed that contract is sitting next to you. Did he know what he was signing, and when did you find out?"

(If Umbra was exposed in act 4, the commissioner names it. On the routes where you traded silence for the name, or bought it, the second sentence stays this vague, and that's enough: the question is about what Gus knew, not what the commission can prove about Wren.)

Three actions, none labelled:

- **Tell the truth.** "He knew. He told me. I used it to get the name back." The conditions name Gus in the record and bar him from ever operating or hosting a licensed floor, which hands Stella her wish as a verdict rather than a choice; he can still come to opening night as a guest and cut the ribbon. The license comes with a year of commission audits. If your act 4 route was black-hat (the payers list, the lifted trust records, leaning on Richard), it comes out here too and the conditions stack. Ed and Graham/Claire count the honesty for you. Gus looks at the mug.
- **Dress it up.** "He was defrauded like everyone else." A bluff on information. Gus holds the fact and says nothing; the lie is told for him, and his pose swap is the only sign of what it costs him. It holds if nobody else in the room can contradict it: Bennett stays silent if your act 4 route was reconcile or by-the-book, and Gus's desk recording of the squeeze never reached evidence. It fails if Bennett is hostile (he traced the camera company himself and squeezed Gus over it, so he knows Gus knew) or if Richard's box went to Ed at A4.4 (the recording is in evidence). Exposing Umbra at A4.3 proves the feeds went to Umbra, not what Gus knew, so on its own it doesn't break the bluff. A failed bluff is the strictest set of conditions in the game, never a lost license.
- **Take it** (only if you heard Gus's confession at A4.1; otherwise you can't claim you knew). "I won't speak for him. I knew before I filed, and I filed anyway." You aren't claiming the deal, which was signed years before you came to Northlight. You're owning the non-disclosure. Gus holds no license (he is retiring and is never on the new one), so the commission has nothing of his to condition, and without your testimony it has no reason to name him. The whole cost of the concealment falls on you: a probation year on your own license (licensed-only play before opening night, a fine, a commission observer on opening night). Gus is never named in the conditions, and he cuts the ribbon. If Rosa is one of your witnesses, she doesn't know the camera story, but she knows what taking a cost for Gus looks like, and her pose swaps once. Gus never thanks you for it out loud; later, on the new floor, he says her name for the first time in years (see S-021).

Sample panel, black: *Nobody at the table looked at Gus. That was the tell.*

**Gus's role afterwards.** He is never an owner or a licensed host of the new Starflare (O1, O2). He retires. The docs' informal roles in the other resting points are unchanged: he hosts the regulars' floor in O4 and greets at the Aurelian in O5. In O1 he comes to opening night as a guest, cuts the ribbon in a jacket that fits, and doesn't play. Stella got what she wanted; the name lived anyway.

## Why it works
The game's founding secret is a woman taking the cost of Gus's weakness at a table. Putting the same choice in the player's hands at the climax is mirroring and shared hardship at once: the player either repeats Rosa's sacrifice knowing what it cost her, tells the truth she couldn't, or lies the way Gus has lied all game. The question is about approving illegal activity on a licensed floor, which is what a gaming commission actually cares about, and it spends existing assets (Wren's servers, Bennett's paperwork, the act 4 routes) as the information that decides whether a bluff holds. It also gives "never fails" teeth: the worst case is a cost you chose to carry. Making Gus's retirement the hearing's outcome resolves Stella's thread without a scene: it isn't a thing she has to win.

## How the player experiences it
Hearing-room scene after the witnesses. Two-shot of you and Gus; pose swap on Gus (chin-up to mug) when the question lands; Rosa's one finger on the table if she's present; a black panel before the answer. The three options are actions with a stake shown ("Take it. (A probation year on your license.)"). Conditions appear afterwards in the Wallet as a line item, like any other debt.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Players who skipped A4.1 never heard Gus's confession, so the commissioner's question needs one line of setup and the truth option reads as the player's guess, the bluff still works, and "take it" isn't offered. The bluff branch needs four states (Bennett friendly/hostile, box handed to Ed or not), all of which already exist as flags. S-021's door scene is where Gus later says Rosa's name; this file works without it, so it isn't listed as a dependency. If Gus is absent or has turned on you (the O2 track, where you bought the name from Wren at A4.3), the question is asked about your own purchase instead and only the first two answers exist. Conflicts with S-002, which made Gus the one who speaks; S-002 was rejected on 2026-10-27.
