## Summary
A 300-word history for Ray and four lines, including the one he says when he shrinks.

## Current state
Mid-40s, leather car coat, gold chain, pinky ring, a car he can't afford; loan shark; answers to Wren; the go-between; the secret drive; makes Kiran skim; voice: too loud, too friendly, lies by flattery (people/ray.md).

## Proposed change
Ray was a Levee kid who ran errands for the card room under the Stacks when it was the old man's, and he was good at exactly one thing: making people feel they were about to be lucky. The old man liked that and kept him around for twenty years as the friendly face of a business with no friends, and Ray mistook being useful for being family. He lent money because lending was being liked for a week. He bought the coat because the old man wore one. He bought the car because the old man's sons did, and when the old man gave the room to the granddaughter with the tablet, Ray was the only person in the building who didn't see it coming, because he'd been watching faces and she'd been watching everything else.

He has worked for Wren for two years and has never once been in a room where she didn't already know what he was about to say. It has made him louder. He lends more than he should because a man with a big book is a man who matters; he squeezes dealers like Kiran because the book has to balance before she sees it, and he tells himself she doesn't see it, because the alternative is that she's letting him. He copied her logs off the Prairie Queen feed one night, drunk and frightened, and hid the drive in his car, and a kid stole it with the rent money. He has not slept properly since.

He is not cruel. He's a salesman who sells the one thing he never had, and he has been selling it to Gus for years in the form of a loan, and the day Gus signed the Flare over to "a friend of mine," Ray drove home and sat in the car for an hour, because he'd just done to the kindest man in the Levee what the family had done to him.

**Sample dialogue.**
- "Buddy! My favourite person. You know what I like about you?"
- (On Gus) "He's a host. Hosts don't say no. That's not me taking advantage, that's hospitality."
- (Blackspruce, the shrink) "...Yeah. No, I'm good. I'm good. You want another drink? I'll get it." (He gets up. He doesn't come back for a while.)
- (If you gave him the drive, once, quietly) "You looked. ... Okay. Okay. Your money's clean with me. Always. Don't make me say it twice."

## Why it works
The history makes the flattery a wound, the car a costume and the drive the one honest act of a dishonest life, which is what S-044 pays off. It keeps him an antagonist (he ruined Gus) while making the ruin something he knows.

## How the player experiences it
Lines, and the hour in the car, which can be a single plate at A2.2 if the player is in the Flare's lot: Ray's car, lights off, a man in it. It rhymes with Bennett's (S-038), which is the point.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Two men sitting in parked cars across two suggestions is a rhyme; if it reads as repetition, keep Bennett's and cut this one.
