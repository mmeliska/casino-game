## Summary
Establish why Gus and Dot haven't spoken since the funeral: the night his wife died, Gus was at the Cinder Room with his phone silenced in his coat, and Dot is the one who couldn't reach him. It is the one thing neither of them will say, and it's under every warning Dot gives you.

## Current state
"They haven't really spoken since the funeral" (people/gus.md, people/dot.md); the reason is not established. Dot "never talks about Gus" and warns you off the Cinder Room among other places (people/dot.md). Gus "is secretly a gambler in the Cinder Room" (people/gus.md). Wren's people had Cinder Room footage of him to blackmail him with before the camera deal, about four years before the game, so the habit predates the game by years (people/gus.md, plan/t08.md). The Cinder Room runs roughly 2am to dawn (plan/t03.md).

## Proposed change
The wound, never told as a paragraph: his wife, Alice (S-013), collapsed at home late on a winter night. Dot called the Flare; Pete said Gus had left after closing. Dot called Gus's phone from two until four. He was under the Stacks, cash on the table, phone in his coat. She died in the ambulance with her sister holding her hand and her husband not there. At the funeral Dot said nothing to him and hasn't since. Gus has never told Stella where he was; Stella thinks he was at the Flare doing the books.

How it shows, without being said:
- **Dot's Cinder Room warning on night one** is personal, and the only warning she gives twice. "I'd stay out from under the Stacks, hon. Nothing good ever came up out of there." (She doesn't lie; she warns sideways.)
- **Gus keeps his phone face-up on the desk** in every office scene, always. An insert, once, early. Nobody explains it.
- **A1.9 "Cracks"**: when you see Gus in the Cinder Room, he's the only player in the room with his phone on the felt.
- **A4.2**: when Dot comes to help him quit, her first line to him in years is the time: "Two ten." He knows what she means. The player doesn't yet. If you told Stella (S-046), she asks later, and that's the reveal (3 or 4 old-photo panels, per the writing rules); if you didn't, Dot gives it to you herself at the picnic table, in one line, and Stella never learns.

## Why it works
Right now the Dot-Gus silence is a "small feel-good thread" with no cause, so reconciling them is cheap. With this under it, every Cinder Room scene carries dread the player can't name, Dot's warnings become love, and A4.2 becomes the scene where two people who lost the same woman say one number to each other. It also explains the gambling as grief, not weakness, which keeps Gus sympathetic through the relapse. (Technique: wound shown through behavior, revealed late, re-reading everything before it.)

## How the player experiences it
Dot's doubled warning, the phone insert, one line at A4.2, and Stella's question as the reveal. Then the player replays night one in their head.

## Risks and tradeoffs
It is the darkest fact in the game; the tone rules allow noir after dark, and this stays off-screen and unspoken. Check it against Dot's "chatty, motherly" voice: she can be both, and the one subject she won't be chatty about is the point. It makes "help him quit" weigh more, which S-015 relies on.
