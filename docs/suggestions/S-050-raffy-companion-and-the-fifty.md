## Summary
Take up the review's companion proposal for Raffy in its cheapest form (a runner who can be sent across the river), and give him one recurring beat: he tries to return your $50 tip, in different ways, until you let him.

## Current state
Raffy: 19, runs envelopes for Ray, stole the wrong envelope, hides at Millstone Lanes; choices at A1.5 include finding him and helping him pay Ray back; the review proposes him as a companion ("a runner: gets messages and cash across the river, finds people"), "Proposals from the review ... not yet settled" (people/raffy.md, pages/review.md). Voice: fast, run-on, lies by talking faster. His mom is a nurse on doubles.

## Proposed change
**The runner.** If you help him pay Ray, Raffy's number appears in Messages. From then on, at a break point, you can text him one of three jobs: carry cash across the river (he's the only way to move money over the Long Bridge before dawn without carrying it yourself; S11's hoodlums never bother a kid on a bike), find someone (he knows which bar the rival's in, which pier Kiran's at), or deliver a thing from Pockets to a person (the gift to Dot, the bobblehead to Gus; never a scene-gated item like Pete's pin). Each costs a few dollars and a slice of the day. He never carries anything from the story-key row; "Not that one. Last time I carried something I didn't know what it was."

**The fifty.** The tip he gave you on night one is the one thing he's ashamed of; it was Ray's money. He tries to give it back: first as a $50 bill left in your motel room, then as a week of free runs, then as a delivery-bike helmet in Pockets ("in case"), then, once, in person, at the Lanes, slowly, without talking fast. If you take it, the arc closes; if you never do, the epilogue's Raffy panel is him with fifty dollars in his hand.

**Badly:** if you keep his $2,000 or hand him to Ray, Kasia tells you his mother called the bar looking for him, and that she told her you were the last one he talked to. Dot stops warning you about the bridge.

## Why it works
The game needs one character the player can act *through*, and a 19-year-old on a bike is the cheapest possible companion: no portrait change, no combat, just texts and a slice of the day. The fifty is a guilt the player didn't ask for, which is the kind that sticks.

## How the player experiences it
Messages tab jobs; items appearing; one scene at the Lanes.

## Risks and tradeoffs
The runner needs a small job-type in the phone (three templates). Carrying cash across the river for you touches the best-bankroll rules only in that the money moves; it never changes its ownership. Keep his jobs cheap and slow so they're a convenience, not an economy.
