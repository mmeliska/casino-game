## Summary
Put numbers on Raffy's envelope so A1.5's "help him pay Ray back" costs the player something: Ray is $500 short, the player covers it, and Raffy pays it back, ending with the fifty-dollar tip from night one. Add a darker A1.5 option: lie to Raffy that it's settled. The review's runner companion was considered and cut (Mike, 2026-10-29).

## Current state
Raffy: 19, runs envelopes for Ray, stole the wrong envelope, hides at Millstone Lanes; choices at A1.5 include finding him and helping him pay Ray back; the review proposes him as a companion ("a runner: gets messages and cash across the river, finds people"), "Proposals from the review ... not yet settled" (people/raffy.md, pages/review.md). Voice: fast, run-on, lies by talking faster. His mom is a nurse on doubles.

## Proposed change
**The envelope's numbers** (Mike, 2026-10-29). The envelope held $2,500 when Raffy lifted it from Ray's car. He took $450 for his mom's rent, which is why he stole it, and tipped you $50 out of it on night one. You hold the other $2,000 (the docs' "about $2,000"). So Ray is $500 short even if every dollar you hold goes back. A1.5's cash choices, with the drive staying in your pocket (S-058's read rule):
- **Return it to Ray.** He gets $2,000. Raffy still owes him $500 and stays in hiding.
- **Help Raffy pay Ray back.** The $2,000 goes to Ray and you cover the missing $500. Raffy is clear and owes you $500.
- **Keep the cash.** As in the docs.
- **The lie.** Keep the $2,000 and tell Raffy, "It's settled. You owe me $500."

**What he owes you** (help path). He can't pay in one go, so he chips away at it: small amounts of cash left for you, and the delivery-bike helmet in Pockets ("in case"). The last payment is the one scene at the Lanes, slowly, without talking fast: "That's all of it. The fifty too. The one I gave you that first night was Ray's. This one's mine." If you tell him to stop paying, he does, but the last scene still comes, with just the fifty. His epilogue panel is him on the bike, debt-free.

**The lie** (Mike, 2026-10-29). Raffy believes he's clear, so he stops hiding at the Lanes. Ray is still looking and finds him within days, so the player collects a payment or two at most. Raffy tells Ray, "They said they paid you." Ray comes to the player's table, friendly as ever: "Buddy. We need to talk about an envelope." The $2,000, plus interest, goes on the player's debt to Ray, and Ray never trusts them again: no lowered interest, and S-044's give-back has no effect beyond the plain one. Raffy disappears. His mom calls Kasia's looking for him, Kasia tells you she said you were the last one he talked to, and Dot stops warning you about the bridge. Ray still never says "drive" (S-058).

**Badly:** if you keep the $2,000 without lying, or tell Ray where he's hiding, Kasia tells you his mother called the bar looking for him, and that she told her you were the last one he talked to. Dot stops warning you about the bridge.

## Why it works
The docs' "help him pay Ray back" had no cost and no number. Now it has both, and the debt gives Raffy a reason to keep turning up after A1.5. The fifty becomes the last piece of it, and the one thing he wants to be clean. The lie gives the dark player a real temptation with a cost that follows from the story, not from a rule: their own lie is what sends Raffy back into Ray's sight.

## How the player experiences it
Four cash choices at A1.5; small repayments and a helmet in Pockets; one scene at the Lanes. On the lie path, Ray at the table: "Buddy. We need to talk about an envelope."

## Risks and tradeoffs
The repayments are cash events, not a new system. The lie path adds $2,000 plus interest to the player's debt to Ray, so it needs the debt screen to show where it came from ("the envelope").
