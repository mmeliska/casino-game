## Summary
State Ray's want (to be the man he's dressed as) and need (to get out from under Wren before she lets him finish digging), add a choice no doc offers (return the drive to Ray, and tell him you looked), and give his epilogue two ends. Updated 2026-10-28 to fit S-058: the drive is the superseded Revaris key ledger Ray kept as insurance, not a copy of Wren's logs.

## Current state
Ray: mid-40s, "trying a bit too hard," answers to Wren (a sore spot), the go-between who never knows the whole plan; made a secret insurance copy of Wren's logs that Raffy stole by mistake (S-058 replaces this: he kept a superseded encrypted key ledger from the Cinder Room's enrollment station, where he runs enrollment for Wren); "can only hunt for it quietly"; "lends too much," makes Kiran skim, "thinks Wren doesn't know. She does, and lets him dig his own hole. He shrinks when the real Umbra shows up" (people/ray.md). A1.5's choices: return the envelope to Ray, keep the cash, hand the drive to Ed, help Raffy pay him back. S11 gives the player leverage on Ray.

## Proposed change
**Want:** to be the car, the coat and the ring. **Need:** out. He kept the old ledger instead of destroying it because some part of him knows Wren will finish him; it's the only smart thing he's ever done, his own name is on it, and he can't tell anyone (S-058).

**The new choice.** Under the read rule (S-058, Mike 2026-10-29), the drive never leaves the player's hands at A1.5: A1.5 settles the envelope's cash and Raffy, and the drive's fate is chosen later. Add one fate: give the drive back to Ray. It is open to any player holding the drive from act 2 on, and it is best after the burn-bag puzzle, because then the player has read it. What the player says depends on what they know:
- Read it (rebuilt the key): "I read it." Ray's name is on page one, and he knows the player has seen it.
- Plugged it in but couldn't open it: "I looked." The player knows it is Revaris hardware from Ray's car, and Ray knows they know.
- Never plugged it in: they just hand it over. Ray takes it and says nothing. He gets only the plain-return effects below.

Pose swap: arms-wide to hands in pockets (the review's tell). He's a different man for one panel. He never thanks you and never forgets. Effects (for "I read it" and "I looked"):
- His loans to you carry no interest, ever. This is the top tier: returning the cash at A1.5 lowers Ray's interest, as the docs' "easier loans" (Mike, 2026-10-29).
- At A4.7 "loose ends," "leverage on Ray" becomes "Ray owes you," which pays as a favor instead of a threat.
- At A4.2, when the player gets Gus out of the Cinder Room, Ray walks him up the stairs to the player's car himself. It's the only decent thing Ray does in the game, and the player caused it. The beat still ends at Dot's lot as settled.
- The dirty route opens through Ray by confidence, not by threat. In act 4 he comes to the player: the two of them lean on the payers together, which is what he kept it for. If the player never read it, Ray opens it for them with his own key.

**Arc stages:**
1. The pitch (act 1): the password, the loan, "buddy."
2. The squeeze (act 2): Wren's crew moves in; he's louder than before, which is the tell.
3. Blackspruce (winter): the real Umbra at the table; he shrinks in front of you; if you hold his secret he's the one who makes sure you leave with your money.
4. Loose ends (act 4): the plate from S11, the skim, the drive, and the burn bag (S-058) if the player took it. He is the most leverageable man in the game and knows it.

**Epilogue, two ends:** if Wren falls (A4.3, by Ed's subpoena if you gave him the drive, or by Felix/Nora's Aurelian footage if the drive went back to Ray), Ray's front page is either a man testifying (if the S11 plate put him at the lawyer's meeting and you gave it to Ed) or a man who left town the week before. If Wren stands, he's still at the Flare's bar in whatever the Flare became, a little older, the car gone, and he says "buddy" to you and means it as a question.

## Why it works
The hub character of the debt system has no mercy in him and none available to the player; adding one choice that treats him as a person gives the loan shark a face and the player a kind of power the game doesn't otherwise offer: knowing something about someone and not using it. (Technique: unexpected mercy, reciprocity.) It also closes a logical gap: a player who has read the drive knows Ray's secret, and the docs never let them say so.

## How the player experiences it
One extra fate for the drive, from act 2 on, with a line that depends on whether the player read it; a pose swap; a quieter Ray afterward; and one scene at A4.2 where the loudest man in the game is the one who gets Gus out.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Giving Ray the drive takes it out of the player's hands for good, and the option's stake should say so ("Hand Ray the drive. You won't get it back."). The costs under S-058:
- Ed's subpoena route at A4.3 loses its probable cause. The Aurelian footage route (Felix/Nora) still exists for exposing Umbra.
- At A4.5 the player can offer Wren only Gus's markers, so O8 is closed and O10 stays open. Wren asks "Where is it?" (S-042).
- A player who read it first keeps what they read: the names, in their notebook. That is enough for leaning on payers, but it isn't evidence and it isn't the device Wren wants (S-058).

A player who gives the drive back trades the clean route and O8 for a friend in Umbra and the dirty route on easy terms. That is a real choice, not a free one.

If the drive was ever plugged into anything online it phoned home (S-058): Wren already knows the old drive survived. Ray getting it back doesn't make him safe. It means she lets him go on digging, which is what his epilogue's "left town the week before" pays off.
