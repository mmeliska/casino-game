## Summary
The first night the player sleeps in the car under the Bridges, one person comes to find them. Who it is depends on who the player has earned. Nobody comes if nobody was earned. It is the game's one use of the most underused attachment technique in the cast: someone showing up for the player.

## Current state
Going broke opens jobs, Ray's loans, Backwater work and staking; "the car under the Bridges" is rock bottom and "broke is content" (pages/gdd.md, pages/direction.md). Every broke-path item is something the player does. No NPC does anything for the player when they're down, except Ray (a loan), Kasia (credit, a debt), Pete (comps, which stop when you cool off) and Dot (a night off rent, for a morning's work). The romance page proposes a partner's couch as a housing step (pages/romance.md).

## Proposed change
A reveal-type event, once per playthrough, firing the first morning after a night in the car (a break point: waking up). The event director picks the highest-standing eligible NPC from an authored list and plays a six-panel scene:

| Who | What they bring | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dot | A room key, a week paid, in an envelope under your wiper, with "You can owe me" on it in her hand. She didn't come herself; Dot doesn't leave her lot (S-016). She's never let anyone owe her in the game | Reliable scale, nudged by her |
| Kasia | A plate of pierogi and your tab, which she has opened again | Her ledger, as kindness |
| Gus | A comped room over a Levee bar (the housing ladder's next step, given, not earned). He changes the subject when you thank him | His only thing to give |
| Pete | Nothing. He stands by the car and says your name, then "The Flare's warm. Nobody checks the lounge." ("Kid" only if S-019's name beat hasn't fired yet.) (Before the fall only; after it: "The Aurelian's warm. I can't comp you. I can not notice you.") | The rule bent |
| Jalen | A coffee and a $20 he can't spare. He says it's his tips. It's the stake you gave him, coming back early | Reciprocity |
| Juan | A cot in the Lanes' back room, "till the sweep's over." The first night, the league bowls around you | Backwater's version of a comp |
| Raffy | Finds you because he's the only one who can; brings your mail from Dot's | The runner |
| A romance partner | Their couch (as the romance page proposes) | The housing step |
| Nobody | The plate: the car, the bridge, morning. No line | What you've earned |

If more than one qualifies, the highest standing comes; the others get one line later ("Heard you were under the bridge. Should've called.").

## Why it works
Every attachment technique in the cast is the player caring for an NPC. The reverse, being cared for, is what makes a cast feel like people rather than quests, and the docs' own pillar ("broke is content") has no scene that delivers it. The "nobody" panel is the sharpest consequence the game can give a tight, flaky player, and it costs nothing.

## How the player experiences it
Wake up in the car, one scene, a change in housing or wallet, and a memory. Later, if the player gets rich, the person who came is the one whose standing the player will find hardest to lose.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Nine short scenes is real writing, but each is six panels on an existing plate. The event must fire only once, or the broke path becomes a kindness farm. The Pete and Gus versions touch the housing ladder (a room over a Levee bar); keep them within the settled steps.
