## Summary
A 350-word history for Wren consistent with the settled takeover timeline, and five lines.

## Current state
Granddaughter of the old Umbra boss; family expected a grandson or an uncle to inherit; left, crypto scams in her early 20s, invented AI face-matching cameras, built Revaris Technologies; landed the Flare's camera deal with her grandfather about four years before the game; he handed her the reins about two years before; kept the dirt on the old bosses and half the Machine; the old guard resent her (people/wren.md).

## Proposed change
Wren grew up in a Silvershore house that Umbra money had bought and Umbra manners had never quite filled. Her grandfather ran the outfit from a card room under the Stacks and from a breakfast table where his sons talked and his granddaughter listened. She was the one who understood the books at twelve and the one nobody asked, and she learned the lesson the family was teaching without meaning to: being right is nothing; being seen to be right is everything; and the only way to be seen is to see first.

She left at eighteen on a scholarship nobody in the family thought was real and made her first money the way clever, angry people did in those years, by building things that took from people who trusted a screen. She was good at it and she did not enjoy it, which she has never admitted. The cameras were different. A face in a crowd, matched to a name, matched to a debt: it was the family business done honestly, which is to say by a machine, with no uncle in the room. She built Revaris to look like what it was, a security firm, and came home when her grandfather got sick, and sold him the one thing he'd never had: a way to know everything without trusting anyone. He took her to the Flare to meet Gus. He shook Gus's hand; when Gus refused, her people showed him the footage, on her tablet, with her in the car outside. It was the last deal he made, and he gave her the room afterward because he finally understood what she'd been for.

The uncles have not forgiven her and she has their faces on file, which is forgiveness enough. She keeps the Cinder Room because it was his, and the bandits because he played them, and she sits at the third one when the room is empty (S-042). She has never been surprised since the breakfast table. She runs a city's cameras and knows the name of every man who owes Ray and the time every alderman meets a lawyer, and she has not once, in two years, been asked a question she couldn't answer. She has built her whole life so that she never will be. Backwater voted her cameras down. She has never been there.

**Sample dialogue.**
- "I already know. I wanted to see if you'd tell me."
- "Ray thinks I don't know about the boat. Ray thinks a lot of things. It's one of his qualities."
- "My uncles wanted the room. I gave them the footage of themselves wanting it. We're very close now."
- "Nothing I have on you is a lie. That's what makes it expensive."
- (Backwater, once) "What's on the drive?"

## Why it works
It reconciles the crypto-scams past, the cameras and the takeover into one motive (to see first) and gives the "never been to Backwater" fact the weight S-042 spends. The lines keep her calm and polite and let the menace come from accuracy.

## How the player experiences it
Lines only. The grandfather landing the deal with Gus is settled (room9.md: they "land the deal together"); the handshake and the car outside are this suggestion's framing.

## Risks and tradeoffs
"Scholarship nobody thought was real" and "came home when he got sick" are new flavour and fit the two-year timeline. Keep her lines rare; four across the game is plenty.
