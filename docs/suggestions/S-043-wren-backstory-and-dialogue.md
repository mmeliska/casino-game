## Summary
A 300-word history for Wren consistent with the settled takeover timeline and with S-042 and S-058, and five lines.

## Current state
Granddaughter of the old Umbra boss; family expected a grandson or an uncle to inherit; left, crypto scams in her early 20s, invented AI face-matching cameras, built Revaris Technologies; landed the Flare's camera deal with her grandfather about four years before the game; he handed her the reins about two years before; kept the dirt on the old bosses and half the Machine; the old guard resent her (people/wren.md).

## Proposed change
Wren grew up in a Silvershore house that Umbra money had bought and Umbra manners had never quite filled. Her grandfather ran the outfit from a breakfast table where his sons talked and his granddaughter listened. She was the one who understood the books at twelve and the one nobody asked, and she learned the lesson the family was teaching without meaning to: being right is nothing; being seen to be right is everything; and the only way to be seen is to see first.

She left at eighteen on a scholarship nobody in the family thought was real and made her first money the way clever, angry people did in those years, by building things that took from people who trusted a screen. She was good at it and she did not enjoy it, which she has never admitted. The cameras were different. A face in a crowd, matched to an ID, matched to a debt: it was the family business done honestly, which is to say by a machine, with no uncle in the room. She built Revaris to look like what it was, a security firm, and came home when her grandfather got sick, and sold him the one thing he'd never had: a way to know everything without trusting anyone. He took her to the Flare to meet Gus. When Gus refused, her people showed him the Cinder Room footage of his gambling, on her tablet, with her in the car outside. It was the last deal her grandfather made himself. Some two years later he gave her the outfit, because he finally understood what she'd been for.

The uncles have not forgiven her and she has their faces on file, which is forgiveness enough. She keeps the Cinder Room because the men who matter in Northlight have always gone there to be unseen, and a room like that is worth more as a listening post than as a card game. She runs it through Ray and goes there as little as she can (S-042, S-058). She built the one system in the city that is blind to her own people, and she has arranged her life so that nobody will ever know who they are but her and the man who holds her pen. She has not once, in two years, been asked a question she couldn't answer.

**Sample dialogue.**
- "I already know. I wanted to see if you'd tell me."
- "My uncles wanted the room. I gave them the footage of themselves wanting it. We're very close now."
- "Nothing I have on you is a lie. That's what makes it expensive."
- (The office, lights going dark) "Now it's just us. I don't do that for many people."
- (Once) "Did you read it?"

## Why it works
It reconciles the crypto-scams past, the cameras and the takeover into one motive (to see first), and it makes the drive (S-058) the exact shape of her one weakness: the single thing she couldn't let the machine see. The lines keep her calm and polite and let the menace come from accuracy.

## How the player experiences it
Lines only, and only one scene. The grandfather landing the deal with Gus is settled (room9.md: they "land the deal together"); the car outside is this suggestion's framing.

## Risks and tradeoffs
"Scholarship nobody thought was real" and "came home when he got sick" are new flavour and fit the two-year timeline. The first draft's line about Ray and the boat is dropped: she knows about Ray's skim and lets him dig (bible 4.1), but she must not appear to know he kept the old ledger until the drive phones home (S-058). Keep her lines rare; five across the game is plenty.
