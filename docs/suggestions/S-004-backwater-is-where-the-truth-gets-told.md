## Summary
Turn the camera dead zone into a writing rule: the scenes where someone tells the truth happen in Backwater or on the Long Bridge. Move Gus's confession (A4.1) to the Slack Water Motel, hold Ed's honest talks at Millstone Lanes, and give Wren one scene where she switches her own cameras off.

## Current state
Revaris cameras cover the city; dead zones are "all of Backwater" and the Long Bridge (pages/gdd.md, people/wren.md). The docs use this for logic: Raffy hides in Backwater, Ray meets the lawyer on the bridge, Umbra must watch room 9 in person. A4.1 "The truth" has no location (pages/storymap-beats.md). Ed's squad runs on the readers and "he knows it and hates it" (people/ed.md). Wren is "rarely seen" and A4.5 is at the Cinder Room, under her own cameras; nothing says what she does with them.

## Proposed change
A one-line rule for writers: **people say what they mean where nothing is recording.** Three applications:

- **A4.1, Gus crosses the river.** Gus has never been "over the water" in the game. He drives to the Slack Water Motel, sits at the picnic table, and tells you he sold his dad's place to the people who blackmailed him. Dot sees him from the office door and does not come out. The player's forgive-or-hold-it choice stays as written. A4.2 ("help him quit, with Dot and Stella") ends on the same lot: you drive Gus over before dawn and Dot speaks to him there (S-016).
- **Ed talks straight at the Lanes.** Every informant-path conversation with Ed happens at Millstone Lanes or Kasia's. His reason, once: "My own squad's readers. I don't talk business where my boss can pull the tape." The honest cop is honest only where his department can't watch him, which is the irony the docs already set up.
- **Wren, once, turns her own cameras off.** At A4.5, in the Cinder Room office, she darkens the four cameras herself before she speaks, and asks the one question in the game she can't check (S-042). She never leaves the Levee; she makes the dead zone with her own hand, which is the rule proved from the other side.

## Why it works
Geography becomes meaning. The cameras are currently a mechanism the player is told about; this makes them something the player feels, because confession, honesty and vulnerability all happen on the dry side, and the Levee's neon is where people perform. It also solves A4.1's missing location with the most loaded place in the game: Gus confessing in front of the sister-in-law he hasn't spoken to since the funeral.

## How the player experiences it
Nothing on screen names the rule. Gus's portrait over the Backwater motel plate is the tell; so is Ed's coffee on Juan's counter. A player who notices that every honest scene happens across the river has found a pattern the game never explains.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Costs almost nothing in art (existing portraits over existing plates). The risk is overuse: if every scene is "let's go to Backwater" the rule becomes a joke. Keep it to confessions and the three scenes above, and let the Levee keep its lies.
