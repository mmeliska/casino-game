## Summary
Explain why a 60-year-old pit boss "can't afford to retire yet": after the funeral, when Gus couldn't work, Pete put his savings into the Flare's payroll to keep it open and never wrote it down. Gus knows. It's the one debt the man who never says "debt" has never acknowledged, and the one Pete will never collect.

## Current state
Pete "has taken Tae's offer because he needs the money and can't afford to retire yet" (people/pete.md); Mike specified "not a pension" (pages/notebook.md). Pete "kept the Flare running after the funeral," is "the only one who tells Gus hard truths," "never compliments Gus to his face," and "lies by not answering." Gus's Ray debt is personal and off the books (people/stella.md). No reason for Pete's finances is given.

## Proposed change
The fact: for most of a year after the funeral, Pete made payroll from his own account on the weeks the Flare was short. He told the staff Gus had covered it. He told Gus nothing. Gus found the deposits later, in the books Stella would eventually run, and has never once brought it up, because the word he'd need is the one he doesn't use. Pete's house is paid off and his savings are a dinner jacket's worth of nothing, which is why Tae's offer isn't a betrayal, it's arithmetic.

How it shows:
- **Pete's rating card.** The card he always holds has, on its back, a column of dates and amounts in his handwriting. An insert, once, when he sets it down at the dark pit (A3.4). No caption.
- **Stella's numbers.** When she "runs it nine ways," one of the ways has a year of deposits she can't source. She reads it as Gus's remortgage (the existing seed). It wasn't.
- **The "lies by not answering" tell.** If the player asks Pete why he's leaving for the Aurelian, he doesn't answer. If they ask again at the dark pit (A3.4), after seeing the card, he says: "Kid. Some numbers you don't say to a man's face." (The one "kid" S-019 allows him after the name beat.)
- **The payoff.** When you give the pin back and hire him home (the existing mechanic), Gus is in the room. Gus says it: "I owe you a year, Petras." First name, full, the one the docs give in parentheses and nobody uses. Pete: "You owe me a coffee." That's the whole scene.

## Why it works
It makes Pete's departure a sacrifice instead of a defection, which is what the dark-pit scene needs to land, and it gives the pin-return scene a line. It mirrors the Flare's whole economy of unspoken markers (Richard's, Rosa's, Gus's IOU to you) in the one man who'd never call one in. It's a secret the player discovers through a prop they've seen all game.

## How the player experiences it
One insert, one line from Stella that re-reads, Pete's non-answer, and Gus's one sentence at the hiring-back.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Another Gus debt; the counter is that this is the shape of the character and the docs already have four. Make sure Stella's misread stays plausible: the deposits are cash, so they look like Gus's money. The "Petras" line uses the name the docs give; if Mike prefers Gus never use it, "Pete" works almost as well.
