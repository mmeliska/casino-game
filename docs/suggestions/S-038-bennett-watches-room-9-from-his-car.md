## Summary
Give the alderman one scene with no speech: late, in the Slack Water lot, a professional-looking man in a parked car with the engine off, watching the door of room 9. He never gets out. The player can see it from the motel steps. It's a clue to M4 and the only time the game shows Bennett's private face.

## Current state
Bennett "pays his motel bill every week by envelope and never visits" (this suggestion changes that to: never visits the room); "never mentions his father"; "a polished public voice and a plain private one" (people/bennett.md, people/richard.md). Umbra watches room 9's door in person because Backwater is a dead zone (pages/room9.md). M4's clues: the envelope drop Dot sees, the photo on Bennett's desk, War with Richard. The private voice has no scene anywhere.

## Proposed change
A random event in the Slack Water lot's pool, before dawn, once per act from act 2, low weight: a dark sedan in the lot with a man in it. Insert: the dashboard, a folded campaign flyer, a hand on the wheel. If the player has seen Bennett's desk photo, the artist note "Bennett's jaw" (already in Richard's look) does the rest. No line.

Three possible player actions, all cheap:
- **Walk past.** The car starts and leaves. The flag is set: `saw_bennett_at_room9`.
- **Knock on the window.** He lowers it an inch. Private voice, plain: "Go to bed." Then he leaves. Standing with Bennett drops a step; the flag is set.
- **Tell Dot.** She's seen the car; she doesn't know whose it is, and she doesn't know Richard's story (the bible keeps her ignorant of both). "Some man. Couple of years now, most Tuesdays. Never gets out. I don't ask, hon." (She doesn't lie; she warns sideways.) The player who has seen the desk photo knows more than she does.

Payoff: in the reconcile route, Richard's first words when his son sits down at the picnic table, before their hand of War, are "You could have knocked." If the player has the flag, the line lands; if not, it's just a father talking.

## Why it works
"Negotiate with, never defeat" needs the player to see what the obstacle costs the man. A politician who pays for his father's room and can't walk twenty feet to the door is the whole character in one plate, and it's shown, not told, which the writing rules demand. It costs one plate (the lot at night with a car) and a line.

## How the player experiences it
A before-dawn random event at the motel, missable, with a tiny inset callback at the room 9 climax. Dot's line is the only commentary.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Umbra also watches the door in person; make sure the player can tell a campaign flyer from a goon (the flyer is the tell). Bennett comes most Tuesdays; the player should catch him rarely (low weight, before dawn only), so "never visits" stays true of the room and the player's sighting feels like luck.
