## Summary
The rags-to-riches arc is frictionless. Add three turns: he loses your first stake and repays it in busboy tips; he gets an offer from Tae's poker room and takes it without telling you; and a bad ending where he climbs with the rival's money instead of yours.

## Current state
"You can stake him, his wins pay you back, and your choices decide whether he climbs clean. He gets out of Backwater, pays back his stake in act 4, and becomes one of only two backers" (people/jalen.md). The review proposed losing him if "you push him to climb dirty, or cut his stake when he's cold" (pages/review.md). Tae hires Pete away at the fall; nothing says Tae notices Jalen.

## Proposed change
**Turn 1, the lost stake (act 1 or 2).** The first time you stake him he loses it all in one session to the rival. He does not ask for more. Over the next two in-game weeks envelopes appear in your Pockets: $20, $35, $15, busboy tips, until the stake is repaid to the dollar. Insert of the last envelope: a note, "Thank you, sir. Next time I'll fold the river." He never says a word about it on the floor. That's the character.

**Turn 2, the offer (act 3, after the scatter).** Tae offers Jalen a prop seat in the Aurelian's poker room. Jalen takes it and doesn't tell you; you find out from Felix/Nora or from seeing him at an Aurelian table in a chain blazer with a house player's badge. His line, when you ask: "I didn't want to ask you first, sir. Asking is how my brother started." (S-027.) The player's choice: let him go (he comes back as a backer anyway, with his own money, which is the clean climb), or stake him to play against the Aurelian's room instead of in it (he comes back as a backer with your money, which binds him to you and gives Tae a reason to resent you).

**Bad ending.** If you cut his stake when he's cold or lean crooked, the rival stakes him instead. Jalen climbs with Chase/Brooke's money and when the rival hits bottom (A3.8) Ray's collector comes for Jalen's winnings too. If you refused the rival cash at A3.8, Jalen is the one who steals from you (A4.8), to cover the rival's debt, because he was staked by them and Ray doesn't care who pays. He leaves the money in an envelope later. Same handwriting.

## Why it works
A feel-good arc needs one real disappointment and one real choice or the player's investment is a bet with no odds. The envelopes are the cheapest emotional mechanic in the game (inventory as character). The offer makes Tae's poaching reach the player's own crew, and the bad ending keeps Jalen likable even as a thief, because the theft is the rule he lives by failing under pressure.

## How the player experiences it
Envelopes in Pockets; a sighting at the Aurelian; one conversation; and, badly, an A4.8 variant with a face the player didn't expect.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Three new short scenes. The A4.8 variant replaces the rival as thief in that branch only (the rival staked Jalen); in every other branch the rival's own theft (S-031) fires instead, never both. "Prop seat at the Aurelian" assumes the Aurelian's poker room exists; the venue table lists poker.
