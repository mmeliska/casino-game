## Summary
Settle what the drive is, why it ties Revaris to Umbra, and how anyone can read it. The drive is Revaris's encrypted key ledger: the only place an anonymous face ID is tied to a name. On its own it proves nothing and can't be opened. In Ed's hands it is probable cause for a subpoena that forces Revaris to show its cameras are blind, citywide, to Umbra's roster and to the men who pay Umbra. The player can also open it themselves, by reassembling the key from a scrap Ray wrote it on, shredded into the burn bag in the Cinder Room office (the GDD's jigsaw mini-game). Worked out with Mike, 2026-10-28.

## Current state
The docs say the drive holds "Ray's insurance copy of Wren's face-match logs, taken off the Prairie Queen camera feed," in two files: the logs, which "prove the Revaris feeds go to Umbra" and are all the clean route uses, and the payers list (people/raffy.md, people/ray.md, audit 4.6). The player opens it at A1.5 and chooses its fate. A4.3 uses it as proof; A4.5 is Wren's deal for it. Nothing explains how a log of face matches proves who owns the camera company, how Ray got it, or why a system like Revaris would put it on a thumb drive.

## Proposed change
**Separation of knowledge.** A Revaris site box stores face templates with anonymous IDs. Its exclusion list is a set of IDs: don't log or alert on these. No box holds a name. The ID-to-name key exists in one place, on a drive that never touches a network, because Wren keeps the one file that could sink her as far from a machine as she can. (It still checks in if someone plugs it into one; see "The drive phones home".)

**Why a key has to exist.** The list changes. Men pay to be unseen and stop paying; the uncles' people come and go. Someone has to know which ID to remove. So there is a ledger, and it is encrypted, and it is carried by hand.

**Enrollment at the Cinder Room.** The room's long tradition of powerful, shady men is why Wren keeps it (Mike, 2026-10-28): intel on those men, and a place to run Umbra's dealings through proxies. She rarely goes there herself. When a man pays to be unseen, he is enrolled there: face captured, ID generated, name and ID written to the ledger. Ray runs the room for her, so Ray is the one who writes to the ledger. Every enrollment means opening the ledger with its key. Wren's rule is that the key lives in his head. Ray can't hold a long string in his head, so he writes it down, in a couple of places. Besides Wren, he is the one person who ever sees a name beside an ID. Wren trusts him with the pen and has made sure he knows nothing else.

**What Ray did.** Wren moved the ledger to a newer, more secure device, with a new key, and told Ray to destroy the old drive. He kept it. Most of the names are still good. That is his insurance against Umbra, and his own name is on it, which is why he can never admit he kept it: a missing drive points at exactly one man. He carried it the way he carries everything, in an envelope in his car with a night's collection cash, and Raffy lifted the envelope (A1.2, as the docs have it).

**What the player sees at A1.5.** They plug it in and get a locked volume with Revaris's name on it and a serial number. They can't open it. They know two things: it is Revaris property, and it was in Ray's car with collection cash. Nothing at A1.5 calls Ray Umbra's man: Dot warns you about "Ray," never about Umbra, and Ray never says who he works for (Mike, 2026-10-29; naming rule, bible 4.1). The player can connect Revaris to Ray, and later Ray to Umbra, but no text does it for them before M2.

**The read rule** (Mike, 2026-10-29). The player can always read the drive once they've solved the puzzle, so the drive never leaves their hands at A1.5. A1.5's choices settle the envelope's cash and Raffy: return the cash to Ray, keep it, or help Raffy pay Ray back. The drive stays with the player whatever they pick. Ray can't ask for it out loud. If anyone in Umbra hears him hunting a Revaris drive, he's finished, so he only ever says "the envelope." When the player returns the cash without the drive, he counts it, looks at them, and says nothing. The drive's fate (Ed, back to Ray in S-044, Wren at A4.5, or keep it) is chosen from act 2 on. The player may still hand it over unread. The docs' A1.5 option "hand the drive to Ed" moves later with it.

**The drive phones home** (Mike, 2026-10-29). Wren keeps the ledger off networks, but it is Revaris hardware, and when it is plugged into anything online it checks in. A decommissioned serial waking up tells Wren two things: the drive was never destroyed, so Ray lied, and someone has it. It doesn't tell her who. Plugging the drive in is therefore a choice with a cost the player can't see. If the player never plugs it into anything online, Wren learns it exists only when Ed starts the subpoena. Ray knows better and opens it only on an offline machine. Wren handles the news her usual way: she lets Ray dig, as she does with the skim, and waits to learn who has it. That waiting ends at A4.5.

**The key is on paper.** The old drive opens only with the old key, the one Ray typed at every enrollment until the upgrade. He had it written down in a couple of places. He still has one copy, so he can open the drive himself. After the upgrade the other scraps looked useless, and at least one went through the office shredder. Wren's rule is cross-cut shredding into a burn bag her people collect weekly. The Cinder Room is the one room she rarely enters, and Ray takes the shortcut: a strip shredder, and a burn bag in the office closet that nobody has collected in months, because collecting it would mean someone checking on him. He writes the new key down too, because he hasn't changed. Her design is sound. Her mistake is Ray. (Settled with Mike, 2026-10-28.)

**Getting the bag.** Three existing doors into that office: working off a debt to Ray on the broke path (the docs already put the player "in the room"), the collect-for-Ray job, or Raffy as a runner (S-050). The bag is a theft and carries heat. If Ray ever works out the bag is gone, he knows which of his people was in the closet, and the player's standing with him drops to the floor.

**The mini-game.** The GDD's tile-rotate/jigsaw for a shredded document, with a twist: the bag holds months of office paper, and among it more than one of Ray's handwritten scraps, with the new key as well as the old. Only the old key opens the drive. The player assembles a handwritten scrap out of a haystack of printed strips, and may have to rebuild more than one to find the right key. The handwriting is Ray's, which tells the player whose secret this is without a line of dialogue. Reassemble enough of the key string and the volume opens. Insert: the first page of the ledger, names beside numbers, Ray's among them, two aldermen, men the player has met. No caption.

**M2's proof** (Mike, 2026-10-29). The docs' M2 clue "Raffy's thumb drive shows the Revaris camera feeds going to Umbra" can't work for a locked ledger. Replace it: if the player has rebuilt Ray's key and opened the drive, the first page (Ray's name, and Cinder Room men the player has met, beside Revaris ID numbers) is the proof. A locked drive plus Revaris buying the Flare at A2.2 is a hint in the notebook, not proof. The broke-path clue (Ray calling the company "us") stays as the other proof.

**Who can read it.** Wren. Revaris, under subpoena. Ray, who still has a copy of the old key. And a player who has rebuilt Ray's shredded copy.

**Why it ties Revaris to Umbra.** A box alone is forty numbers that mean nothing. The ledger alone is forty names beside numbers that prove nothing. Put a judge between them and Revaris must produce what its system does with those IDs: nothing. It is configured, citywide, not to see them. A security company blind to a mob roster and to men who pay the mob.

**The routes, revised.**
- **Clean (A4.3):** hand Ed the drive, opened or not. Encrypted, it is still probable cause: Revaris-serialized hardware in a mob collector's car, with Raffy's statement. Opened, it is stronger. Either way Ed needs a subpoena, and the encryption works for him: the ciphertext is in evidence, so Wren can produce the real key or refuse, and refusing is its own answer. The route is slow and loud, because a subpoena to Revaris tells Revaris, and Ed can't serve it through his own squad, whose readers are Wren's (S-048). He goes around his department to the commission or the state. Umbra and the payers go down together, and some of the payers didn't deserve it, which is the clean route's cost.
- **Dirty:** the payers' names are only useful to someone who can read them. Either the player rebuilt the key, or Ray opens it for them (hold his debt over him, or earn his confidence), and the two of them lean on the payers together, which is what Ray wanted the insurance for all along.
- **Ruthless (A4.5):** give the device back to Wren. O8, as settled. She never knows whether it was read.

**What Wren is afraid of.** Not the player reading it. Ed holding it. Once the drive is in evidence she can't make it say anything else. So A4.5 is the deal she makes to stop A4.3 from happening, and the only person who can give her the drive is someone who may have no idea what's on it.

## Why it works
A smart adversary's system fails the way smart systems fail: at the one human it had to trust. Every piece is realistic (an air-gapped ledger, a password on a scrap of paper, strip shredders, subpoenaed configuration) and every piece is a character beat: Wren's separation of knowledge, Ray's shortcut, Ed's compromised department. The drive is a sealed thing in act 1, a puzzle in act 2 or 3, and a clock Wren can't see in act 4. Opening it shows the player what the Cinder Room is without a line of exposition.

## How the player experiences it
A locked volume at A1.5 that stays in their pocket; a closet, a bag and a jigsaw if they go looking; a page of names; and three ways to spend it.

## Risks and tradeoffs
Changes the docs: `people/ray.md` and `people/raffy.md` lose "copied the logs"; the drive is one encrypted file, not two; A4.3's clean proof becomes "Ed subpoenas Revaris"; Ray's Cinder Room role gains "runs enrollment for Wren." The bag theft needs one authored event and the jigsaw needs a "haystack" difficulty setting and handwritten tiles. Two clauses lean on proposed suggestions, kept in prose rather than depends_on: Ed's squad running on Wren's readers (S-048) and Raffy as a runner into the office (S-050). If either is rejected, drop that clause; the rest stands. Keep the ledger page to names and numbers; never show the system's internals on screen. Nothing player-facing may name Umbra as Revaris before M2 (bible 4.1): the locked volume says Revaris, the envelope says Ray, and the player draws the line themselves.
