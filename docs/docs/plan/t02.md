**Goal:** give the city a name and a distinct identity.

**Already decided:** a big fictional Great Lakes city, Chicago-like, not desert. A river flows into the lake and splits the city. Gambling became legal on one side, at first only on the water; across the river, Backwater stayed dry by local vote (same city and state; changed 2026-10-17). Districts: the dry side across the river, the bridges, the river district (old downtown), the lakefront strip, and the gold coast. Seasons matter. Backdrop: machine politics, old mob history, union power, old money versus new. Working name (2026-10-16): Northlight. Runner-up: Breakwater. District names: Backwater (the dry side across the river); the Bridges; the Marquee Mile (lakefront strip, a nod to the Magnificent Mile); Silvershore (old money, a nod to the Gold Coast); the Levee (old downtown on the river where the Starflare and riverboats sit, a nod to Chicago's old vice district). Names should avoid real places and other games, films and books (the Narrows was dropped because of Batman). Phones: full smartphones, chosen over phones banned on the casino floor and flip phones only. Default until revisited: online casino betting is illegal in the state, so you still have to show up to gamble. History (no dates, era-agnostic): Northlight was a steel, rail and shipping town; when the steel mills closed and the waterfront emptied, the state legalized riverboat gambling "on the water only" to bring jobs back, as Gary and East Chicago really did. Land casinos came later, and that is when the Starflare went from a rocket-age supper club to a licensed casino (the illegal back room was dropped 2026-10-22 so it isn't confused with Kasia's). Backwater is dry by local vote (Mike, 2026-10-17, replacing the earlier "other state" setup): the state allows gaming only in licensed districts (on the water first, then the Levee, the Marquee Mile, Silvershore's private clubs, and a few slot licenses at the Bridges), each district voted on whether to allow licenses, and Backwater's proud mill families, wary of the casinos, voted no. LICENSE RULE (Mike, 2026-10-26, audit 2.5): the license follows the name. The commission treats the Starflare as one licensee changing address, so winning back the name lets it be licensed again wherever it reopens in a licensed district (the lakefront on the Marquee Mile at the Silvershore line, a boat on the water, or a union hall in the Levee), after a commission hearing on the player's record (A5.7). Who licenses (Mike, 2026-10-30): the state gaming commission grants every casino license; the alderman only gives the local sign-off (zoning and permits). The player owns the new Starflare and holds its license; O3 and O4 each get a short hearing, and A5.7 is the full version. So every game in Backwater is illegal, and one Northlight vice squad covers the whole city, working out of Lock Street Station (Mike, 2026-10-23; Ed's base). Cameras (Mike, 2026-10-30): Backwater voted only on casinos, not cameras. Its streets have the city's "Safe Streets" plate readers and most of its shops took Revaris cameras below worth. Three places refuse them, each for its own reasons and with no tie between them: Kasia's Tap, Millstone Lanes and the Slack Water Motel. The Long Bridge stays dark (the hoodlums smash its readers). The irony that the district that voted against gambling has the most underground gambling gives Bennett a talking point. Precedent: Chicago's dry precincts; no religion in the game, so as not to put off players. Who runs the city: the Machine (mayor and ward aldermen trading licenses and permits for votes and payoffs; the Levee's alderman matters most to the Flare), Umbra (working name "the Families", then Umbra; briefly Argus, then Revaris and the Black Hand on 2026-10-23; back to Umbra on 2026-10-25 by Mike. Its front is Wren's security camera firm, Revaris Technologies. NAMING RULE (Mike, 2026-10-25, audit 2.1): the outfit is Umbra to everyone, on the street and inside; Revaris Technologies is only Wren's camera company, a respectable-looking tech firm. Nobody outside the outfit knows Revaris is Umbra's front, and that link is the secret Gus hides (M2) and the room 9 box proves (M4), so player-facing text never ties the two before M2 is solved. Umbra is the old mob behind the pre-legal back rooms, now skimming through loans, vending and security; tied to Ray and the mob-takeover ending), the Unions (dealers' and dockworkers' locals; tied to Rosa), and new money versus old (out-of-town chains on the Marquee Mile, Silvershore families owning the land; tied to the lakefront rebuild). Names for the mayor, Umbra's bosses and the Silvershore families wait for the cast task. Housing ladder: you start at a weekly motel in Backwater; go broke and you sleep in your car under the Bridges; moving up, a room over a bar in the Levee, then a comped suite on the Marquee Mile (lost when you stop betting big), then a place in Silvershore (bought, or as someone's guest). Landmarks and lore: the North Light, the old harbor lighthouse the city is named for (when it flickers, the house runs cold); the Long Bridge, the main crossing with gas-station slots (never carry winnings across it before dawn); the Stacks, dead mill chimneys along the Levee; the Starflare's rocket sign, and the chrome spaceman by its doors (regulars rub his boot for luck). Slang: "riding the light" (hot streak), "gone dark" (broke), "going over the water" (heading to Backwater). Food: lake perch fry, steelworker pierogi, and the Stacker, a sausage-and-pepper sandwich from the mill-shift days.
