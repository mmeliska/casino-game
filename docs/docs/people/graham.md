**First met:** the club car of the Northern Ledger on your first spring trip to the state capital. **Value: integrity with power.** Either version is a statehouse staffer who knows how gaming licenses really get decided, and has watched powerful people trade favors for years. They live in Northlight the rest of the year (settled), so chance meetings in the city are possible, and they hear about your year through the news and statehouse gossip.

**Story role: a by-the-book way to win back the name (act 4).** Besides the room 9 box or A4.3 (handing the proof to Ed, or trading your silence to Wren) (there is no cash route, 2026-10-23), they know a license rule: the commission can take an alderman's local sign-off over a license away if he has a conflict of interest, and Bennett takes a hidden cut of the stadium deal. **Together:** they walk you through it and get your complaint in front of the right people, so you can win back the name by the rules without exposing Gus's Umbra deal. They also set the tone of the epilogue headline about Bennett's mayor race, though not who wins (Mike, 2026-10-30): the commission finding his hidden cut is a public exposure, so on this route Mayor Dabareski is re-elected. **Friend:** they explain the rule, but you have to file and push the complaint yourself, at a real cost in money and time. Either way the proof is lawful (audit 4.3): Mitch names the filing lawyer's trust account that carries Bennett's cut, the complaint cites it, and the commission subpoenas the records. **Ended:** you never hear about it.

**Exclusive route:** the conflict-of-interest route above is this romance's exclusive. It only reaches you through a statehouse insider who trusts you; without Graham/Claire, the name comes back only through the room 9 box or A4.3 (handing the proof to Ed, or trading your silence to Wren); there is no cash route.
