## Backstory

Gus's grown daughter, a finance analyst downtown. She learned to count at the cage: her mother, Alice, ran the drop with Stella on a stool beside the window, and the first numbers she ever said out loud were stacks of twenties called back to her mother (Mike, 2026-10-29). The Flare was where she did homework, had birthdays and learned the band's songs through the office door. She was ten when her grandfather died and twelve when her mother died. She was told her father was doing the books that night; she has never asked, and she decided then that someone in the family should actually do them.

She went downstate on a scholarship and came back to a desk downtown with a view of the Stacks, close enough to run the Flare's numbers and far enough not to work there. Gus keeps the Flare's books himself, badly. She asks for copies every quarter and runs them at her kitchen table; she has never been on the payroll. She has run them nine ways, and every way loses. She took the figures to Tae because he always let her count as a kid, and because she needed someone to tell her the number was real (Mike, 2026-10-29). She was at school during payroll night, and the books never showed it, because Rosa dealt Gus back to even (Mike, 2026-10-29).

She never sees the Ray debt (audit 3.8): Ray lends to Gus personally, off the Flare's books, so she sees only operating losses, the roof and the thin crowds. One thing doesn't add up: Gus quietly remortgaged his house. She reads it as him propping up the Flare with his own money, which is part of why she wants him to let go. When the truth comes out, she realizes what the mortgage was really for. She thinks the lounge's star ceiling is the most beautiful thing in Northlight, and that her mother got one constellation wrong on purpose; it's the only thing she's ever said to her father about her mother that made him laugh. Dot is her aunt and Pete is "Uncle" Pete. Tae was like a big brother to her when she was little, which is why she trusts him.

## Role

Loves her dad and thinks the Flare is killing him, so she wants him to sell. She wants to keep him; what she needs is to stop running him nine ways. She quietly meets Tae at the Aurelian, which makes her a false lead in M2; after the sale she has asked him to dig into who's really behind Revaris Technologies, because the deal smells wrong to her and the chain knows the industry (audit 3.9), so her meetings mirror your own search. Clearing her lets you compare notes and brings her and Gus closer. She is the human face of the buyout and closure endings. Her "he should retire" thread resolves without a scene: Gus is never an owner or licensed host of the new Starflare, and he retires (Mike, 2026-10-27).

## Arc

1. **The nine ways** (acts 1 and 2): figures, worry as health, the meetings with Tae, the false lead. In act 2 or 3 she tells you about the remortgage as proof he's propping up the Flare. She's wrong, and you may know it.
2. **The last night** (A3.3): one of the six scenes. At the cage she counts the drop down for the last time, out loud, and stops before the total. "You don't need the number." You say it for her, or don't.
3. **When she learns** (Mike, 2026-10-27, OQ 33). She learns about the gambling from you, if you choose to tell her: "Tell Stella" is a third option at A4.1, after Gus's confession. If you tell her, she joins the A4.2 reunion at Dot's lot that morning, so Dot, Gus and Stella are together once: "Dad. ... How much." He gives her the number; she already knows the rest from you. She doesn't write it down. The torn patch then leaves her speechless for a different reason: someone else paid for it years ago and he let them. If you never tell her, the reunion is Dot alone and she learns at the torn patch, speechless. Gus never tells her himself. Telling her also spends Wren's old leash on Gus (the footage threatened at Stella), which helps push Wren to make her A4.5 offer (Mike, 2026-10-29).
4. **The hearing** (A5.7): if she heard it from you and not from the patch, she can speak for you, and for the first time she speaks in stories, not figures (Mike, 2026-10-29).
5. **Opening night** (O1): Gus is retired and a guest. She's at the new cage with the first night's drop in front of her, the count sheet blank. She closes the ledger without writing the total. To Gus: "It's enough." That's the only number she says all night.

In O6 (Gus lets go) she comes to the visits at the Slack Water picnic table without her laptop, nothing in her hands. That ending is hers more than anyone's. A4.6 (backing Tae's offer) leads to O5.
