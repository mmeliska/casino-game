*Supporting (story) · Backwater · room 9 at the Slack Water Motel*

## Look

Late 70s and a little worn down: thin white hair that isn't always combed, grey stubble most days, tired eyes, moves slowly. Dressed very modestly in a faded flannel shirt, worn work trousers, a thrift-store cardigan with a missing button and scuffed shoes. Optional artist note: Bennett's jaw, so the photo on Bennett's desk clicks when you meet him.

## Backstory

Bennett's father (Mike's pick for the name). A few years ago he ran up markers at the Flare and the house he'd lived in for decades went to cover them. Bennett forced Gus to wipe out the rest. Ashamed, Gus gave him the key to a safety deposit box at an old Levee bank, rented in Richard's name so it opens only with his signature, holding the original signed camera contract and Gus's own recording of Bennett's demand (the only copy; the office is off the floor cameras), where he spells out the trade: "I'll keep quiet about who your cameras belong to." Gus told Richard: "If anyone ever comes after you over the debt, open it." He wears the key on a lanyard and has never opened the box; he keeps his word and is too proud to take Gus's help. Gus chose the motel, not Bennett: he arranged the room through Pete, his go-between with Dot (they barely speak), and paid the first weeks. Bennett found out and took over the bill by envelope out of pride, because he won't let Gus pay for his father. Dot only knows Pete asked a favor. It's in Backwater, a camera dead zone, which is why Umbra has to watch the door in person (audit 4.8). Bennett pays his motel bill every week by envelope and never visits. Richard doesn't know: he thinks Gus pays it out of guilt. Umbra watches his door to see if he ever goes to the bank (the key is useless to them without his signature), because the box proves Wren's camera company, Revaris Technologies, is Umbra's front.

## Role

Plays War alone at the picnic table in the motel lot, and you can join him (a story-event game: one pass, 10 cards each, standard War ties). It's the game he taught Bennett as a boy. Once he trusts you, umbra men, who followed you there because you've been digging, knock on room 9 with Richard's old Flare markers: Revaris bought the Flare, so it owns the marker books, and they say the write-off was never legal, so he owes them now. Pay, or hand over the key. That is someone coming after him over the debt, so he opens the box with you, and he has the final say: shame Bennett into amending his stadium ordinance to return the name to Gus, give Bennett the box and reconcile father and son (they play a hand of War; out of gratitude Bennett files the same amendment), burn it, or hand it to Ed (which exposes Gus's deal too). This is one of the leverage routes to winning back the name.

## Voice

Slow, sparing with words, a little dry, with pride under the tiredness. Talks about card games, the old Levee and his son as a boy, never Bennett as a grown man; never mentions the debt, the house or the key. Lies with "It's nothing" and a hand over the key. "War's an honest game. No choices, no bluffing. Just the cards." (Settled 2026-10-23.)
