## Backstory

Bennett's father (Mike's pick for the name). He ran a crane on the Levee docks for thirty-eight years and bought a house four blocks from the Flare, because it was the brightest thing on the river and his wife liked the band. He taught his boy War at the kitchen table and let him win, and has never decided whether that was the right thing (Mike, 2026-10-28).

He was not a gambler. He was a man who liked a room with people in it, and when his wife died the Flare was the room, and the cards were the way you stayed in it past the point where company was free. Pete knew him by name; Gus comped his coffee. The markers came a hundred at a time, and he signed them because nobody at the Flare had ever called one in. Then the commission's new rules came, the markers were real paper, and the house he'd lived in for decades was the only thing that covered them. He moved out with a suitcase and a deck of cards. He did not ask his son for anything, because his son had become a man who was against things, and Richard was one of them now (Mike, 2026-10-28).

Bennett forced Gus to wipe out the rest of the debt. Ashamed, Gus gave Richard the key to a safety deposit box at an old Levee bank, rented in Richard's name so it opens only with his signature, holding the original signed camera contract and Gus's own recording of Bennett's demand (the only copy; the office is off the floor cameras), where he spells out the trade: "I'll keep quiet about who your cameras belong to." Gus told Richard: "If anyone ever comes after you over the debt, open it." Richard took the key because refusing would have been a conversation. He wears it on a lanyard and has never opened the box; he has never needed to, and need is the only reason he'd allow. He keeps his word and is too proud to take Gus's help.

Gus chose the motel, not Bennett: he arranged the room through Pete, his go-between with Dot (they barely speak), and paid the first weeks. Bennett found out and took over the bill by envelope out of pride, because he won't let Gus pay for his father. Dot only knows Pete asked a favor. Bennett pays the bill every week by envelope and never visits the room, though most Tuesdays he sits in his car in the lot and doesn't get out (Mike, 2026-10-28). Richard thinks Gus pays the room out of guilt.

Backwater's streets have the city's plate readers, but the Slack Water refuses cameras for its guests' privacy, so Umbra has to watch room 9's door in person (Mike, 2026-10-30). They watch to see if he ever goes to the bank (the key is useless to them without his signature), because the box proves Wren's camera company, Revaris Technologies, is Umbra's front. The street readers log Bennett's plate on his Tuesday drives, so Wren knows Richard is his father; the box is still Richard's and Gus's secret (Mike, 2026-10-30).

## Want and trust

**Want:** to be left alone with the cards and his word. **Need:** to be needed by his son for something other than silence (Mike, 2026-10-28).

**The trust rule, for writers:** Richard's standing never rises when the player gives him something (money, food, a ride). It rises when the player asks him for something: a game, a rule, the story of a hand, how to shuffle one-handed. Offer to pay his room and he says "It's nothing" and the standing drops. Ask him to teach you War and it climbs. It is the opposite of every other NPC's economy, and nothing on screen explains it (Mike, 2026-10-28).

## Role

Plays War alone at the picnic table in the motel lot, and you can join him (a story-event game: one pass, 10 cards each, standard War ties). War is the game with no choices in it, and he's made enough. It's the game he taught Bennett as a boy. First met at A3.7.

Once he trusts you, Umbra men, who followed you there because you've been digging, knock on room 9 with Richard's old Flare markers: Revaris bought the Flare, so it owns the marker books, and they say the write-off was never legal, so he owes them now. Pay, or hand over the key. That is someone coming after him over the debt, so he opens the box with you, and he has the final say: shame Bennett into amending his stadium ordinance to return the name to Gus; give Bennett the box and reconcile father and son; burn it (this road closes); or hand it to Ed (which exposes Gus's deal too). In the reconcile, his first words when his son sits down at the picnic table are "You could have knocked." Then they play a hand of War, and out of gratitude Bennett files the same amendment (Mike, 2026-10-28). No peek and no thrown hand (Mike, 2026-10-27). This is one of the leverage routes to winning back the name. The dirty version is leaning on Richard for the key; he and Dot cool on you.

## Voice

Slow, sparing with words, a little dry, with pride under the tiredness. Talks about card games, the old Levee and his son as a boy, never Bennett as a grown man; never mentions the debt, the house or the key. Lies with "It's nothing" and a hand over the key. "War's an honest game. No choices, no bluffing. Just the cards." (Settled 2026-10-23.) When the Umbra men come, "It's nothing" is the one lie he admits (Mike, 2026-10-28).
