*Core · the Levee · Starflare floor and office; coffee under the rocket sign*

## Look

Very heavyset (Teddy from Bob's Burgers without the beanie, as an artist reference only), silver hair combed back, his dad's old Starflare dinner jacket straining at the buttons, a chipped coffee mug with the rocket logo.

## Backstory

Late 50s, the son of the founder, Mr. Hamilton, who built the Starflare as a rocket-age supper club. Gus was nearly born there: his mother went into labor at a supper-club table, and his dad finished the set before he drove her. He grew up under the planetarium ceiling, doing homework at the bar while Pete, three years older and already busing, taught him to count a rack by eye. He was the owner's boy, so everyone was kind and nobody was straight with him, and he learned to fill a silence with a story about the old days. When the license came, his dad ran the floor and Gus ran the room: comps, birthdays, the band's bar tab.

He married Alice, Dot's younger sister, the winter the piano arrived (Mike, 2026-10-27). She picked the piano, repainted the lounge's star ceiling with one constellation wrong on purpose, and told him he was a host, not a gambler, and should stay one. His dad died about fifteen years before the game, when Stella was ten, and left him the Flare, the dinner jacket and a stack of markers nobody had ever called in.

He started going under the Stacks to the Cinder Room after a bad year. Casino war suited him: no choices, nothing to be good at. The night Alice died he was at a war table with his phone silenced in his coat. Dot called the Flare, and Pete said he'd left after closing; she called his phone from two until four, and Alice died in the ambulance with Dot holding her hand (S-012, Mike 2026-10-27). He chipped the rocket mug that night when he finally looked at the phone, and has drunk from the chip ever since. Stella, who was twelve, was told he was doing the books; he has never told her otherwise. After the funeral Dot asked him, through Pete, for one thing, her mother's recipe box, which had gone to Alice when she married. He said no, because a box on his shelf meant Alice hadn't finished leaving, and the silence became a break (S-011, Mike 2026-10-28).

About six years before the game, drunk, he lost the staff payroll at Rosa's table; she dealt him back to even, the overhead camera saw her hands and not his face, and he let her take the fall. He kept the only wide-angle tape, and he has not said her name since. The commission's penalty ordered a modern camera system he couldn't afford. About four years before the game Wren offered hers for a fraction of the cost; when he refused, her people showed him Cinder Room footage of himself at a war table and threatened to send it to Stella and the commission, so he signed knowing it was Umbra. The cameras cost him far less than they were worth because the real payment is the data. When Bennett found out, Gus had to wipe out Richard's remaining markers; he recorded the squeeze on a desk recorder, put it with the contract in a box rented in Richard's name, gave Richard the key (M4), and arranged room 9 through Pete. He borrows from Ray personally, off the Flare's books, and has quietly remortgaged his house.

By the time you arrive he is the kindest man in the Levee and hasn't finished a sentence in years. Every true thing about him is held by someone else: the footage by Wren, the key by Richard, Alice's name by Dot, the numbers by Stella, and after the fall, the tape by Bennett.

## Personality

Proud, sentimental, stubborn, bad with money. He wants to keep the Flare as his father left it and his wife decorated it. What he needs is to stop being the keeper of the dead: the jacket was his dad's, the piano and the ceiling were Alice's, the pin is his dad's gift to Pete. Nothing in the Flare is his except the habit that's killing it. He gives you coffee because it's the only thing in the building that's his to give. His phone sits face-up on the office desk in every scene; nobody explains it.

## Story

You meet him on your first Flare visit (coffee, rubbing the spaceman's boot) and care builds slowly from small things. The photo of the two sisters is on his office shelf, with a tin recipe box under it (an insert, no caption). At A1.9 you see him in the Cinder Room, the only player with his phone on the felt. Ray's first visit to his office is a warning that the markers are due (A1.10); the collection comes later, in act 2 (A2.1). A "friend of Ray's" buys the Flare and clears his debt (really Revaris Technologies; Gus signs knowing it's the mob and, ashamed, keeps the buyer secret. Umbra, through Revaris, then puts the land into the Levee Stadium District, with Bennett pushing it through). The sale clears all his old debts to Ray; your S6 IOU, if you hold it, is the only paper that survives, because he owes it to you (Mike, 2026-10-29).

He doesn't believe their promise to keep the Flare open and keep him on; he tells himself he does, and says it out loud to the regulars ("nothing's changing, they gave me their word"), because the alternative is admitting he sold his dad's place to the people who blackmailed him. Pressed, he changes the subject. A3.1 breaks the lie (audit 3.11).

On the Flare's last night (A3.3) he is in the office boxing the photo of the sisters, and the recipe box goes in the same carton. Ask about the box and he changes the subject; or help him tape the carton shut. That night he presses one of the Flare's own chips into your hand, his lucky chip. After the closing it can't be cashed anywhere; it's worth nothing except to him, and Troy buys it for almost nothing (Mike, 2026-10-30).

At A4.1 he crosses the river for the first time in the game and confesses at the Slack Water picnic table, where nothing records: he sold his dad's place to the people who blackmailed him. Dot watches from the office door and doesn't come out. You forgive him, hold it over him, or tell Stella (Mike, 2026-10-27). At A4.2 he is gambling harder than ever; you get him out of the Cinder Room (Ray walks him up the stairs himself if you gave Ray the drive back) and drive him over the Long Bridge before dawn to Dot's lot, where she says "Two ten," her first words to him since the funeral (Mike, 2026-10-28). If you told Stella, she is there too.

At A4.5 Wren offers the opposite of A4.2: "Bring him to the Cinder Room. Keep him playing. I'll hold the paper." At his lowest, Gus signs new markers to Umbra against his remortgaged house, and you are the one who put him at the table; your S6 IOU goes into the pile if you hold it. If he has already quit, her offer is the drive only. She needs him silent because he signed the camera deal and the sale knowing who she was, her old leash on him is spent (he holds no license after the sale, and Stella may already know), and solving M1 would put him in front of the commission (Mike, 2026-10-29). On the clean Ed route the sale is undone only if Gus testifies that he was blackmailed, admitting in public that he gambled and signed knowing; that needs his A4.1 confession and his trust. If Wren returns the name early to stop the case, he is spared the testimony (Mike, 2026-10-30).

Winning the Starflare name back for him by leverage, clean or dirty, is act 4. His trust is ordinary NPC standing plus a few flag checks.

**His turn is the recipe box** (Mike, 2026-10-28). In act 5, once he has quit (A4.2) and R1 has progress, he drives over the water on his own and puts the box on Dot's desk: "It was always half yours. I should have said so at the funeral." It's the first scene where he finishes a sentence.

**Act 5 and the endings.** Gus never owns the new Starflare and is never its licensed host; he retires (Mike, 2026-10-27). In O1, when act 5 opens, he signs the name he got back over to you in one short scene (Mike, 2026-10-30). At the hearing (A5.7) he sits beside you as the man who signed the camera contract, called to attend, not to apply, and the hard question is about him: did he know what he was signing, and when did you find out. Telling the truth names him in the record and bars him from ever running or hosting a licensed floor; dressing it up is a lie told for him while he says nothing; "take it" (only if you heard his confession) keeps him out of the record. He never thanks you out loud. On opening night he is a guest: he cuts the ribbon in a jacket that fits and doesn't play, and the old jacket hangs in the new lounge by the piano. When Rosa stops at the door, he says "Rosa." Nothing else. He says "Alice" once, at the end. In O4 he hosts the regulars' floor; in O5 he greets at the Aurelian. In O6 he quits, makes up with Dot and Stella and stops fighting for the name; the visits are at the Slack Water picnic table, and later a sober Gus can ask you to reopen (Mike, 2026-10-29). In O10 you sold him to Wren.

**The bad ending inside the good one.** If you never did A4.2 and he never turned, O1 still happens, but Gus is unchanged: a proud man in a dead man's jacket at somebody else's opening. The post-game has one scene of Gus "out for a walk" at 2am, with the recipe box still on the seat of his car; you can follow him or not, and following him can start the help-him-quit thread late. The Flare got its name back; Gus didn't.

## Voice

Long, warm, nostalgic sentences that trail off. Talks about "my dad's place"; never says the word "debt". Says "she" and "my wife," and says "Alice" exactly once, at the end. Never says Rosa's name until the door. Nobody calls him Mr. Hamilton; if anyone tries, he says "That was my dad." Lies by changing the subject to the old days. *"You should've seen this floor when the band played. Anyway. Coffee?"*
