## Backstory

The loan shark and your easiest big debt. A little too confident; acts like the big man but answers to Wren, which is a sore spot. Renamed from Dutch (clashed with Dutch Schultz and Red Dead Redemption 2); read Ray wherever older notes say Dutch.

A Levee kid who ran errands for the Cinder Room when it was the old Umbra boss's, and stayed twenty years. He was good at one thing: making people feel they were about to be lucky. The old man kept him around as the friendly face of a business with no friends, and Ray mistook being useful for being family. He lent money because lending was being liked for a week. He bought the coat because the old man wore one and the car because the uncles drove one. When the old man handed the outfit to his granddaughter, Ray was the only one in the building who didn't see it coming (Mike, 2026-10-29).

He has worked for Wren for two years and has never been in a room where she didn't already know what he was about to say. It has made him louder. He runs the Cinder Room for her, and she gave him the one job that puts a name beside a number: when a man pays to be unseen by Revaris's cameras, Ray enrolls him at the station in the room's back office, typing the ledger's key each time. Besides Wren, he is the only person who ever sees a name beside an ID (Mike, 2026-10-28). When she moved the ledger to a newer device and told him to destroy the old drive, he kept it, on purpose, as insurance: the one cold, sober decision of his life. Most of the names are still good, and his own is on it, so he can never admit he kept it. He still has the old key written down, because he has never in his life remembered a number he didn't owe. He carried the drive the way he carries everything, in an envelope in his car with a night's collection cash, and Raffy lifted the envelope (A1.2). He has not slept properly since (Mike, 2026-10-29).

He is not cruel. He's a salesman who sells the one thing he never had. The day Gus signed the Flare over to "a friend of mine," Ray sat in his car in the Flare's lot for an hour, because he'd just done to the kindest man in the Levee what the family had done to him. That hour is backstory, never a plate (Mike, 2026-10-29).

## What he knows

He knows Revaris is Umbra's company, because he enrolls the Cinder Room men in its system. When he calls the company "us" while you work off a debt to him (an M2 clue), that is honest; when he swears at A2.2 that he "just knows a guy, never met him," that is a lie, and the S11 plate proves it. What he never knows is the land plan: the Stadium District, Bennett's hidden cut and why the Flare's land mattered. He learns that when M2 is solved, like everyone else (Mike, 2026-10-29).

Want: to be the car, the coat and the ring. Need: out, before Wren lets him finish digging (Mike, 2026-10-29).

## Story

He is first on screen at A1.5, asking around Kasia's and the Slack Water about "the envelope" (Mike, 2026-10-30). He can never say "drive": if anyone in Umbra hears him hunting a Revaris drive, he's finished. If you return the cash without the drive, he counts it, looks at you, and says nothing. Returning the cash lowers his interest on your loans. If you lie to Raffy that it's settled and keep the $2,000, Ray finds Raffy within days, comes to your table ("Buddy. We need to talk about an envelope."), puts the $2,000 plus interest on your debt, and never trusts you again (Mike, 2026-10-29).

He recruits new money for the Cinder Room: once you've won at the Flare in act 1 he slips you the password, "a real game, for real players" (A1.9; audit 4.2). Once you're in, you stay in, even if he later stops trusting you (Mike, 2026-10-30). In your first winter as a Cinder Room regular he invites you to Blackspruce Lodge as his stake partner, once your best bankroll reaches about $25,000 (X4; Mike, 2026-10-30).

He collects on Gus's debt in two visits. A1.10, which closes act 1, is a warning in Gus's office that the markers are due; A2.1, days or weeks later, is the collection where you choose (Mike, 2026-10-29). He squeezes Gus until he sells and brings him Umbra's offer as "a friend of mine" (2026-10-23). The sale clears Gus's old debts to Ray. He took a secret finder's fee from the filing lawyer behind Wren's back, on the Long Bridge before dawn (S11).

He lends too much (to Chase or Brooke, say) and makes indebted dealers like Kiran skim the Prairie Queen's cheap tables to balance his book before Wren sees it. He tells himself she doesn't see it. She does, and lets him dig his own hole.

The drive phones home: if you ever plug it into anything online, Wren learns a decommissioned serial is alive, so the old drive was never destroyed and Ray lied. She doesn't learn who has it, and she lets Ray go on digging (Mike, 2026-10-29). Ray himself only opens it on an offline machine.

His burn bag is your way into the drive. After the upgrade his other copies of the old key looked useless, and at least one went through his strip shredder into a burn bag in the office closet that nobody has collected in months. He writes the new key down too, because he hasn't changed. You can reach the closet by working off a debt to him on the broke path or through the collect-for-Ray job; taking the bag is a theft with heat, and if Ray ever works out it's gone, he knows which of his people was in the closet and his trust in you drops to the floor (Mike, 2026-10-28).

## Arc

1. **The pitch** (act 1): the password, the loan, "buddy."
2. **The squeeze** (act 2): Wren's crew moves in; he gets louder, which is the tell.
3. **Blackspruce** (winter): the real Umbra at the table, and he shrinks in front of you. If you hold his secret, he's the one who makes sure you leave with your money.
4. **Loose ends** (act 4): the S11 plate, the skim, the drive, and the burn bag if you took it. He is the most leverageable man in the game and knows it.

**Giving the drive back** (Mike, 2026-10-29). From act 2 on, any player holding the drive can hand it to Ray ("Hand Ray the drive. You won't get it back."). What you say depends on what you know: "I read it" if you rebuilt the key and opened it, "I looked" if you only plugged it in, nothing if you never did. Pose swap: arms wide to hands in pockets. He never thanks you and never forgets. For "I read it" or "I looked":
- His loans to you carry no interest, ever.
- At A4.7 "leverage on Ray" becomes "Ray owes you," a favor instead of a threat.
- At A4.2 he walks Gus up the Cinder Room stairs to your car himself. It's the only decent thing he does in the game. The beat still ends at Dot's lot.
- In act 4 he comes to you, and the two of you lean on the payers together, which is what he kept the drive for. If you never read it, he opens it for you with his own key.

A silent hand-over gets only the effects of a plain return. If you lied to Raffy, the give-back gets nothing more than that either. The costs: no drive for Ed's subpoena at A4.3 (the Aurelian footage still exists), and no O8, because at A4.5 you can offer Wren only Gus's markers; she asks "Where is it?" If you read the drive first, you keep the names in your notebook: enough to lean on payers, but not evidence, and not the device Wren wants. If the drive ever phoned home, getting it back doesn't make him safe.

**Ends.** If Wren falls (Ed's subpoena, or the Aurelian footage), his front page is a man testifying (if the S11 plate put him at the lawyer's meeting and you gave it to Ed) or a man who left town the week before. If Wren stands, he's still at the bar in whatever the Flare became, a little older, the car gone, and he says "buddy" to you and means it as a question. On the Ed route, if you take Wren's early offer, she blames him in public ("a former contractor kept data he was told to destroy," which is true). Ray takes the fall, the Cinder Room goes dark, and the epilogue shows what it cost him (Mike, 2026-10-30).
