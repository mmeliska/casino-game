## Backstory

Owns the Slack Water Motel and works the desk herself. Early 60s, a few years older than Gus; sympathetic and in the know (Mike, 2026-10-29). She and her younger sister, Alice, grew up in a Backwater two-flat. Their father ran the hoist at the Backwater grain elevator and bowled Tuesdays at the alley that became Millstone Lanes; their mother taught both girls to cook, the two of them standing on chairs to reach the counter. Her recipe box went to Alice at the wedding. Dot was the older one, the practical one, the one who stayed; Alice was the one who went over the water and married Gus, so Dot is Gus's sister-in-law and Stella's aunt. Dot bought the Slack Water with the settlement after the elevator accident that killed their father, and runs it the way he ran his crew: everyone fed, everyone's business their own (Mike 2026-10-28).

She was never a gambler. She played the state lottery once a year on her father's birthday, and one year it hit: enough for a trip. She took Alice, because Gus wouldn't leave the Flare for a week, and they went somewhere warm for nine days. Alice bought two loud shirts and made Dot wear hers on the plane home. Dot has worn it every day since the funeral. It isn't a souvenir of the win; it's the last thing her sister picked out.

The night Alice died, Dot called the Flare (Pete said Gus had left after closing) and called Gus's phone from two until four. Alice died in the ambulance with Dot holding her hand and her husband not there. Dot said nothing to him at the funeral. Afterward she asked him, through Pete, for one thing, their mother's recipe box, and he said no; that refusal made the silence a break. She hasn't crossed the river since the funeral. That they're family is a reveal (a framed photo of the two sisters in Gus's office), never part of her intro.

## Personality

She wants her people safe on her side of the river, where the only thing that can hurt them is weather; every warning she gives is that want talking. She believes, without ever saying it, that the Levee took her sister. The warmest person in the game takes an envelope every week for a man whose story she has never asked, and warns everyone off the Cinder Room without knowing the same outfit watches her own lot. She tells herself room 9 is none of her business, the same way Gus tells himself the Cinder Room is nothing. The Slack Water is one of three places in Backwater that refuse Revaris cameras, for her guests' privacy (Mike, 2026-10-30).

## Her stories

The flood (she let everyone sleep upstairs free and the dockworkers brought food, hence her soft spot for union people like Rosa); the man in room 9 (a quiet guest who has lived there the last couple of years; his bill comes in an envelope every week, she doesn't know his story, and he never takes off a key on a lanyard around his neck; he is Richard, and Umbra watches his door in person); and, once she trusts you, the shirt, told in three or four old-photo panels: two women on a beach, one in each shirt. Back at the desk she adds one line: "She had better taste than me. I got the loud one." She never says who "she" is. After the shirt story she mentions the recipe box: "Our mother's recipes. Alice and me used to stand on chairs to reach the counter. He's got the box. I asked once. Through Pete." She doesn't ask you to fetch it.

She says "Alice" freely once she trusts you. She doesn't talk about Gus: once a season she asks you how he is and changes the subject before you answer, and she says "he," never his name, until she speaks to him at her lot. If you ask about the car in her lot before dawn: "Some man. Couple of years now, most Tuesdays. Never gets out. I don't ask, hon." (It's Bennett's; she doesn't know that.)

## Her warnings

Ray's loans, the dice behind Millstone Lanes when the vice squad is sweeping, the Cinder Room, and carrying winnings over the Long Bridge before dawn. The Cinder Room warning is personal, and the only one she gives twice: "I'd stay out from under the Stacks, hon. Nothing good ever came up out of there." If you keep Raffy's $2,000 or give him up to Ray, she stops warning you about the bridge.

## Arc

- **A4.1:** Gus crosses the river and confesses to you at her picnic table. She watches from the office door and doesn't come out.
- **A4.2, the turn** (Mike 2026-10-28): you get Gus out of the Cinder Room and drive him over the Long Bridge before dawn. Dot is at the car when it pulls in and says "Two ten," her first words to him since the funeral. He knows what she means; you don't yet. If you told Stella at A4.1, Stella joins them in the lot that morning; she asks later what it meant, and that's the reveal, in three or four old-photo panels. If you didn't, Dot tells you herself at the picnic table, in one line, and Stella never learns.
- **Act 5:** once Gus has quit, he drives over on his own and puts the recipe box on her desk: "It was always half yours. I should have said so at the funeral." It pays off R1.
- **Opening night:** she crosses the Long Bridge for the first time since the funeral, in the shirt. In O1 she goes to find the ceiling Alice painted; under the new floor's lights Gus knows which trip the shirt is from (a pose swap, no line). In O2 she comes anyway and leaves before the ribbon. In O6 she doesn't need to cross: Gus comes to her, and the visits are at the picnic table.
- **Badly:** if you sell Gus out (O10), she tears your page out of the register. You can still rent a room; you're a guest now.

Her desk isn't a rule: she comes out from behind it whenever a scene needs her. The motel's scenes are Richard's War games, A4.1, the A4.2 reunion and the O6 visits; don't add others without a reason tied to Dot or Richard (Mike, 2026-10-29).
