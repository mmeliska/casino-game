## Backstory

A tech genius and the old Umbra boss's granddaughter, whom nobody took seriously; the family expected a grandson or one of the uncles to inherit. She grew up in a Silvershore house that Umbra money had bought, at a breakfast table where her grandfather's sons talked and she listened. She understood the books at twelve and nobody asked her. The lesson she took: being right is nothing, being seen to be right is everything, and the only way to be seen is to see first (Mike, 2026-10-28).

She left at eighteen on a scholarship nobody in the family thought was real and made her first money by exploiting people through technology (crypto scams in her early 20s). She was good at it and didn't enjoy it, which she has never admitted. The cameras were different: a face matched to an ID matched to a debt was the family business done by a machine, with no uncle in the room. She invented her own AI face-matching cameras and built a legitimate-looking security company around them, Revaris Technologies. About four years before the game she came home when her grandfather got sick and sold him a way to know everything without trusting anyone. Together they landed the Flare's camera deal: she offered the cameras well below their worth, and when Gus refused, her people showed him Cinder Room footage of his gambling on her tablet, with her in the car outside, and threatened to send it to Stella and the commission. He signed knowing it was Umbra. It was the last deal her grandfather made himself (Mike, 2026-10-28). The data it brought in, from the Flare and then the RailYard and the Prairie Queen, was worth more than anything the uncles ran, so about two years before the game her grandfather handed her the reins over their heads. He died within a year. Nobody else in her family is named (Mike, 2026-10-29).

She kept what her cameras had caught on the old bosses and half the Machine, which is why nobody pushes back (Mike, 2026-10-25). The uncles still exist offstage and have not forgiven her; she has their faces on file. The outfit is still called Umbra; Revaris Technologies keeps its own name and looks like a respectable tech firm, and nobody outside knows it's Umbra's front (Mike, 2026-10-25).

She keeps the Cinder Room because the powerful, shady men of Northlight have always gathered there: it is her intel source on those men and the place Umbra's dealings run through proxies. Not sentiment. She runs it through Ray and rarely goes there herself (Mike, 2026-10-28).

Revaris Technologies buys the Flare from Gus and puts its land into the Levee Stadium District, making it the District's biggest investor: a clean asset for her, and her cameras in the stadium (2026-10-23).

Want: to be the one who knows, so that nobody can surprise her again the way her family did. Need: to be asked something by someone who isn't afraid of her, and not know the answer (Mike, 2026-10-28).

## The business

High-end AI face-matching cameras installed in the Flare, the RailYard and the Prairie Queen, and from act 2 the Aurelian (S13), at a price well below their worth, because the real payment is the data they record. Every feed also goes to her. CAMERA RULE (Mike, 2026-10-26): every venue keeps its own copy of its footage too; Wren can wipe her servers but never a venue's copy, so footage is never something she can simply erase. Her real business is blackmail: gamblers hiding losses, dealers, cops and officials pay her in money, favors or silence. She has dirt on Bennett taking casino money. She pays his hidden cut of the stadium deal herself, through Revaris, so her own books are the proof and she owns the man running for mayor. She never saw Bennett squeeze Gus in his office (off the floor cameras); only Gus's own recording in Richard's box did, which is why she wants it.

THE LEDGER (Mike, 2026-10-28): a Revaris site box stores faces under anonymous IDs, and its exclusion list (don't log or alert on these IDs) is Umbra's roster plus men who pay Umbra to be unseen. No box holds a name. The one place an ID is tied to a name is an encrypted key ledger kept off any network and carried by hand. Men are enrolled at the Cinder Room, and Ray, who runs the room, is the one who writes to the ledger. She built the one system in the city that is blind to her own people, and nobody knows who they are but her and the man who holds her pen. She has not once, in two years, been asked a question she couldn't answer (Mike, 2026-10-28). She moved the ledger to a newer device with a new key and told Ray to destroy the old drive. He kept it. Her design is sound; her mistake is Ray. The old drive is Revaris hardware and phones home: plugged into anything online, it tells her a decommissioned serial is alive, so Ray lied and someone has it, but not who. THE NETWORK (Mike, 2026-10-26; a parallel to real-world plate-reader networks, never named in game): Revaris cameras aren't only in casinos. Bars, shops, motels and parking lots across Northlight take them below worth too (the payment is the data), and a city contract puts Revaris plate readers on streets and bridges, sold to the Machine as "Safe Streets": Bennett champions it while running on "clean up Northlight", the mayor signed it, and Ed's vice squad uses the data, which is how Wren reaches into the police.
