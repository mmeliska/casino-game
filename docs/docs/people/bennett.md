## Look

Early 50s, very professional (Mike). A photo of his father on his office desk is a clue in M4. In the motel lot he is only a hand on the wheel and a folded campaign flyer on the dashboard; if you've seen the desk photo, the jaw gives him away (Mike, 2026-10-28).

## Backstory

The Levee's alderman and ward boss. Genuinely loves the Levee and plays every room for his own gain. Publicly against gambling, but takes bribes from the casino business, which makes him a hypocrite.

He grew up in the house his father would later lose: a Levee two-story four blocks from the Flare, bought on a crane operator's wages when the Levee still had cranes. His father Richard taught him War at the kitchen table and let him win. Bennett peeked at the top card, and by ten he understood that being let win was a kind of being lied to, and resolved to win for real. He did: the ward office at twenty-two, alderman at thirty-one, the youngest the Levee had elected. He learned the Machine from the inside: a license is a favor, a permit is a vote, and a man who says he's against something can take its money as long as he says it loudly enough. He has said it loudly for twenty years and it has never felt like a lie, because he is against it (Mike, 2026-10-28).

About thirty years ago, barely twenty and running errands for the ward office, he told a dozen Levee and Backwater people that Backwater would vote yes on gaming. Mitch bought a Backwater storefront on that tip, and Backwater voted no. The filing lawyer handled those sales. That was the bad tip (Mike, 2026-10-29).

Richard started going to the Flare after Bennett's mother died, for the company and then for the cards. Bennett didn't know until the house was gone. He found out from a county filing, and his first move was not to go to his father but to the records: who held the markers, who signed off on the Flare, who had put cameras in it below cost. Ed gave him the Umbra link in good faith. Bennett used it the way he uses everything, as leverage, and forced Gus to wipe out what Richard still owed. Then he found Gus had already arranged Richard's room, and that is the insult he has never forgiven: the man who took the house also paid the rent. So he took over the envelope. He pays the room every week and never goes in. Most Tuesdays he sits in his car in the motel lot with the engine off and doesn't knock (Mike, 2026-10-28).

## The plan

He doesn't buy the Flare himself (changed 2026-10-23). A stadium company, the Levee Stadium District, gets the Flare's land from Revaris Technologies, which bought the Flare from Gus and is now the District's biggest investor. The state gaming commission grants every casino license; as alderman, Bennett gives only the local sign-off (zoning and permits) for the Flare, which is how he pushes the condemnation through (Mike, 2026-10-30). He sponsors the council ordinance that creates Starflare Field and grants the District the site and the Starflare name (so only he can amend the name back out, or the commission can void that clause; audit 2.6), and takes Umbra campaign money and a hidden cut of the stadium deal, which Wren pays him herself through Revaris, so her own books are the proof and her hold on him. The filing lawyer is Revaris's lawyer; the player glimpses him meeting Bennett at the Aurelian the week the purchase goes through (the M2 clue, act 2), and the Aurelian's archive footage of the same meeting is the A4.3 proof (Mike, 2026-10-30).

He wants a domed baseball stadium, Starflare Field, to revive the Levee, and runs for mayor on "clean up Northlight" against Mayor Dabareski while taking the same money. He is on posters and bar TVs from act 2 and first in person at A2.6 (Mike, 2026-10-30). Why an anti-gambling candidate keeps a casino's name (audit 3.5): Revaris, as the District's biggest investor, insists the District hold the Starflare name, and Bennett sells it to voters with one stump-speech line: "The Starflare was a supper club on our block long before it was a casino. We're keeping the Levee's light, not its dice." Privately, he wants the thing that took his father's house to hold the ball games his father never got to see. He has never said that to anyone. The District restores the old rocket sign and hangs it over the Field gate as a heritage piece (Mike, 2026-10-29).

Starflare Field is the house rebuilt as a civic project: a Levee where his father couldn't have lost anything, with a dome on it. He knows Revaris is Umbra and tells himself the stadium is worth it. When the Flare is cleared, his city inspectors do the condemnation walk-through, which gives his people lawful access to Gus's office; they find Gus's wide-angle tape of Rosa's table and he keeps it secret. An obstacle you negotiate with, never defeat. He also championed the city's "Safe Streets" contract that put Revaris plate readers on Northlight's streets and bridges, cameras everywhere in the name of cleaning up the city (Mike, 2026-10-26). Those readers log his plate on his Tuesday drives to the motel lot, so Wren knows Richard is his father; she keeps it to herself as one more hold on him. Room 9's box is still Richard's and Gus's secret (Mike, 2026-10-30).

## Proposal

He keeps the tape as insurance (audit 3.4): if Gus ever testifies about the room 9 squeeze, Bennett answers with proof that Gus gambled drunk on his own floor and let a dealer cheat for him. He would never use it for his campaign, because a Flare scandal would taint his own stadium. Any act 4 leverage route can trade for the tape as part of its deal; Miles/Audrey are only the easiest way (Mike, 2026-10-27).

## Arc

**Want:** a Levee with a dome over it and no tables in it, so no old man can ever again sign his house away at a felt. Everything he says in public is this want in a suit. **Need:** to stop punishing a building for what his father did at it. The Flare didn't take Richard's house; Richard did, and Bennett has built a stadium rather than say so. **Contradiction:** he keeps the Levee's light, not its dice, and takes the dice's money to do it. He knows. The slogan is the lie he tells with speeches; the envelope at Dot's desk is the truth he tells with cash (Mike, 2026-10-28).

**The car in the lot (M4 clue).** A random event in the Slack Water lot's pool, before dawn, once per act from act 2, low weight: a dark sedan with a man in it, engine off, watching room 9's door. He never gets out. No line unless you make one (Mike, 2026-10-28):
- **Walk past.** The car starts and leaves. Sets `saw_bennett_at_room9`.
- **Knock on the window.** He lowers it an inch: "Go to bed." Then he leaves. Standing with Bennett drops a step; sets the flag.
- **Tell Dot.** She's seen the car but doesn't know whose it is: "Some man. Couple of years now, most Tuesdays. Never gets out. I don't ask, hon."

The campaign flyer is how the player tells him from the Umbra men who also watch the door.

**The mayor's race (R6).** Bennett wins unless he is publicly exposed in act 4: the commission finds his hidden cut (A4.11), room 9 shames him, or Ed's case names him. Then Mayor Dabareski is re-elected. A room 9 reconcile means he wins with a kinder headline. Graham/Claire's choices set only the headline's tone (Mike, 2026-10-30).

**By act 4 route** (Mike, 2026-10-28, with the race rule above):
- **Room 9, reconcile:** his father's first words when he sits down at the picnic table are "You could have knocked" (it lands if the player has the flag). He wins the mayor's race.
- **Room 9, shame:** he amends under threat. At the hearing he's silent and hostile. He loses the race by a few points and is back as alderman in the epilogue, bitter, still paying the room.
- **By the book (A4.11):** the commission voids the name clause. He is embarrassed in public for the first time in his career and, oddly, relieved. He loses narrowly, with a line about "a conflict of interest." He sends Richard's envelope with a note for the first time.
- **The Ed route (A4.3):** if his cut comes out with Wren's books, he is the only person in the game who can go to prison, and whether he does is the epilogue's front page. If Ed's case exposes Umbra but never names him, he survives, weakened.
- **Badly for the player:** if you signed his petition (S7) and fought dirty at A3.2, he reads your name at the council hearing with Gus in the room, and at the license hearing (A5.7) his hostility sets the strictest conditions the game offers. A hostile Bennett also breaks a bluff at the hearing's hard question: he traced the camera company himself, so he knows Gus knew (Mike, 2026-10-27). He never raises his voice.
