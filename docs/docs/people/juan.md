*Supporting · Backwater · Millstone Lanes and its back lot*

## Look

Early 40s, Mexican-American, dark stubble, a grease-stained work shirt with his name patch, a bowling-alley logo cap, a rag in his back pocket.

## Backstory

Started as a teenager clearing jammed pins and learned the machines from the old mechanic, so he's been there about 25 years. About ten years ago the old owner, ready to retire, sold him the alley on a handshake payment plan, and the dice in the back lot after the last frame paid for it (Mike, 2026-10-30). The late game still keeps the ancient pinsetters running. He owns the place and still does his own repairs.

## Personality

Friendly with league bowlers, indifferent to everyone else, and annoyed by casuals. Keeps his game fair, knows every Backwater rumor, and Ed leaves him alone. Wants the pinsetters running one more season. Needs to admit the Lanes are a casino with a bowling problem (Mike, 2026-10-29).

## Story

You meet him when Kasia sends you over with the Tuesday league's free-drink coupons, and he's the one who tells you about the Starflare across the river (A1.3). Raffy hides out with him. Millstone Lanes refuses Revaris cameras, for the late game and his regulars (Mike, 2026-10-30), so it's one of the few places where people say what they mean: Ed talks straight there. What happens inside reaches Wren only through her people, the Umbra crew included.

**The mirror, said once** (Mike, 2026-10-29). The first time you come back to the Lanes after meeting Gus, out in the back lot after the last frame, he says the one thing he's never said to anyone. He knows of Gus, not Gus himself: "Gus runs a casino that wishes it was a supper club. I run a bowling alley that's a dice game. Same thing."

**R3.** Umbra tries to muscle in with loaded dice, and you can help him push them out; as a machine guy he can teach you to spot a weighted die. The stake is stated in parts: "They take the late game, the parts money goes with it. Lane six is dead by the first snow." If R3 fails, lane six goes dark and the league bowls on five.

**The hearing (A3.2).** The league in their shirts (S1) are the Lanes' regulars standing up for the Flare's. Juan doesn't come; he's under a machine. Ask why: "Somebody's gotta keep the lights on where I am."

**Badly:** if you bring heat to the Lanes (a raid traced to you), the late game stops, the parts money stops, and next time there's a sign in the window about the pinsetters. He's still there. He doesn't say "Couldn't tell you." He says nothing.

## Voice

Friendly and easy with league bowlers, flat and indifferent with everyone else; talks while he works. Respects serious bowlers and grumbles about casuals who don't know the rules and leave a mess on the lanes (Mike's real-world detail). Never says "dice" inside the alley; it's "the late game". Lies with a shrug and "Couldn't tell you." Bowling in the Tuesday league is how you warm him up. "Lane six is running dry tonight. Move left two boards." (Settled 2026-10-23.)
