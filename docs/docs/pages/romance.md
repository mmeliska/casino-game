## How romance works

**Rules:** no dating system (no meters, gifts or date button), romance never touches the odds or fairness of the games, and it never involves the main cast. Each romance is a small arc that opens only if you lean in. All six are in their 30s.

**In short (settled with Mike 2026-10-23):** a set of direct and observed choices decides each romance, with the test choice counting double and one deal-breaker possible; each romance plays a real part in the story with one exclusive route, none required; the listed place is only where you first meet them; you can pursue more than one, but a partner who finds out counts it strongly against you; an ended romance can always be rebuilt with later choices; and the landing adapts to wherever the story rests. Beats, choices and voices (still proposals) are on each character's page.

Everything on this page and in each romance character's *Romance arc* section is a **proposal** unless marked settled.

**Settled rules (Mike):**

- No dating system: no meters, gifts or date button. Romance never touches the odds or fairness of the games.
- Never with the main cast. Only the three new characters, each with a male and a female version set by who you dance with at the wedding you work at Kasia's Tap (opposite-sex offer first, from the gender set at Dot's register; the same-sex offer only after a no).
- Your preference is shown by what you do at the wedding (A1.4), never asked. You work the reception for Kasia as a server or busser; she brings the opposite-sex dance offer first (from the box at Dot's register: Mr., Ms. or Prefer not to answer), and the same-sex offer only after a no. You can turn down both (Mike, 2026-10-23). For Prefer not to answer, both offers come at once and you pick one or neither (Mike, 2026-10-30).
- Both offers use the same words and tone. No one jokes about or remarks on your choice, and saying no costs nothing (Mike).
- A set of choices decides whether each romance succeeds or fails (2026-10-23). Some choices the character presents to you directly; others they observe, or hear about, and they form an opinion of you from them.
- The listed place is only where you first meet them (2026-10-23). Dates and chance meetings happen anywhere in Northlight.

**Proposed structure for each arc:**

- **One value per character**, tied to who they are: loyalty (Miles/Audrey), honesty (Felix/Nora), integrity with power (Graham/Claire). Every choice that counts touches that value.
- **Direct choices (2 or 3):** asked in their own scenes. These weigh the most.
- **Observed choices (3 or 4):** choices you make in the main story that they could plausibly see or hear about: they were there, it happened somewhere they go, it made the news, or someone they know told them. If they couldn't know, it doesn't count.
- **Five beats:** meet, a first date, an opening-up scene, a test (the hardest direct choice), and the landing.
- **Hidden tally:** each counted choice is for, against or neutral. The landing reads the tally: mostly for, you end up together; mixed, they stay a friend; mostly against, they end it, but enough "for" choices later can slowly reopen it (settled).
- **Each romance plays a real part in the story (Mike, 2026-10-23).** Each one opens a story route or clue the player can't get as well any other way. The deeper the relationship, the more they help: together gives the full route, a friend gives a smaller tip, and an ended romance gives nothing. Each romance also has one exclusive route that only that character can open (Mike, 2026-10-23). None of them is required: the main story can still be finished without any romance, just by a harder or slower road, which gives players a reason to try a romance on a second playthrough.
- **Reactions show, numbers don't.** A swapped pose and one line after an observed choice ("I heard what you did for Pete") is the only feedback.
- **Epilogue:** a partner appears in your panel of the \~8-panel epilogue; a friend gets a smaller mention.

## Deeper romance (Mike approved the ideas, 2026-10-23)

The six ideas below are settled as directions; the specifics under each are proposals. A shared game with each partner was considered and dropped (Mike: unnecessary).

1. **They want something of their own.** Each has a personal goal you can help with, at a real cost in money or time, and helping is a direct choice. Miles/Audrey wants to record an album of the Flare's old songs. Felix/Nora is saving for night school to get out of hotel work. Graham/Claire is being asked to run for office.
2. **Texts on the phone between beats.** Invitations, reactions to the news, "heard what you did." Your replies count as small direct choices and fill the gaps between the five big scenes.
3. **They react to your bankroll.** If you go broke or end up under the Bridges, they notice. Together, they offer you their couch as a step on the housing ladder; later, moving in together can be a step of its own.
4. **They can be used against you.** Like Jalen in S10, Wren or Ray can lean on your partner to get to you. Protecting them is a test choice with a real cost.
5. **Your friends weigh in.** Dot, Pete or Kasia give their opinion of your partner, and later you meet their family, so the relationship becomes part of the city.
6. **Callbacks.** They remember small things, like your song, your drink and a bad night, and bring them up later.
